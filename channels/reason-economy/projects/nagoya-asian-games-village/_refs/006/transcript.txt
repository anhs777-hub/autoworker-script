The Asian Games organizing committee abandoned the construction of a new athlete's village, located 30 minutes by car from the main stadium, as costs were expected to exceed 500 billion won . This site was originally home to a horse racing track. Demolition was scheduled to make way for the village, but the plan was scrapped due to rising construction costs. Instead, they spent 40 billion won to rent a massive cruise ship with 1,500 cabins to accommodate 4,000 athletes. They are also using 200 wooden modular container units as athlete accommodations. However, even this is insufficient, and the basketball team, which started their matches first, is complaining about the cramped conditions inside. It's very uncomfortable. I'm over two meters tall, so my legs hit the walls and I have to hunch over to shower; if they could provide a hotel, I'd appreciate it. When record-breaking downpours hit Nagoya last week, water leaked from sinks, and athletes in container units had to be evacuated due to concerns over potential flooding. Travel time has also emerged as a variable affecting condition management . Athletes not staying on the cruise ship or in the containers are scattered across nearby accommodations, with the baseball and shooting teams facing travel times of over an hour to get to their venues. It took over an hour to get to the practice gym today, which was a bit frustrating, so the key will be making sure it doesn't affect our performance. Japan's bold choice to forgo an athlete's village for cost-cutting and eco-friendly goals. With poor conditions and safety concerns being raised, the decision is now being put to the test.
