The Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games, which have officially begun, drew attention by claiming to be an eco-friendly event that minimized new construction, but they have lost face by being embroiled in controversies over poor preparation long before the opening. Athletes are staying in existing hotels, modular containers, and cruise ships. Some athletes have been assigned rooms with beds shorter than their height or faced facility issues like bursting bathroom pipes, and events that disregard historical symbolism have also come under fire. A character of Toyotomi Hideyoshi, who instigated the Imjin War, appeared at a welcoming event on the cruise ship Costa Serena used for lodging; the Organizing Committee claims he is a local historical figure, but they are facing criticism for lack of consideration for neighboring countries, undermining the tournament’s goal of peace and harmony, and justifying past history of aggression. In particular, there is criticism of hypocrisy, given that Japan raised issues with a political message five years ago during the Tokyo Olympics when our team hung a banner with a quote from Admiral Yi Sun-sin on the wall of the athletes' village. Poor tournament operations have also come under fire. Our national soccer team's vehicle went to the wrong place—a baseball stadium—causing the team to arrive 30 minutes late, and the Vietnamese women's volleyball team was wrongly directed to their hotel and had to walk to their accommodation. Amidst criticism that there is a lack of basic support for athletes and even a lack of consideration for participating countries, there are calls for a serious change in perspective from the Organizing Committee. This is Woo Joon-sun for Yonhap News TV .
