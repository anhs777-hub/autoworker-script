The shark expert I interviewed estimated this shark to be a blacktip shark belonging to the requiem shark family. It was initially guessed to be a bull shark, but bull sharks only grow up to 3 meters, whereas this one is 3.5 meters, so the possibility of it being a blacktip shark, which grows up to 4 meters, is higher. Both species rarely attack humans first, but it is known that they can be dangerous if provoked. The reason the shark cannot leave the waterway has not yet been clearly identified. There are various interpretations, such as the shark entering the waterway while chasing prey and failing to find the exit, or that there is still prey inside, or that it is staying in the shallows because it is injured. There is also speculation that it might be about to give birth, and rumors of people seeing a 1-meter-long baby shark have even spread, but this has not been confirmed . Experts advise that if signs of the shark's movements slowing down or weakening appear, human intervention should be considered. However, for now, we are waiting for the shark to return to the sea on its own. The Chuseok holiday begins in a few days. If the shark is still here by then, it is expected to be a fascinating sight for those returning to their hometowns. Reporting from Busan, this is Ko Hyun for Yonhap News TV.
