In 1973, Japan's GDP per capita was significantly higher than South Korea's . Over 50 years have passed since then. The world has completely changed. The IMF projects South Korea's GDP per capita at $ 43,512 this year, while Japan remains in the $ 35,000 range. Moreover, there is one more figure in this IMF forecast to keep an eye on. It estimates that South Korea's potential growth rate, which had been declining, will rebound for two consecutive years for the first time in 40 years. This means the Korean economy is currently driving in the passing lane. How did South Korea, which once had a GDP per capita one-tenth that of Japan, manage to overtake it? Can this overtaking continue into the future? And do these numbers have any relevance to the state of my own wallet? On October 14, an IMF Asia-Pacific press conference was held in Bangkok, Thailand. A reporter from the major Japanese economic newspaper Nikkei asked a question. How does the effect of AI on the Japanese economy compare to that of South Korea? The answer returned was that South Korea is one of the countries that has benefited most from AI. >> Japan also benefits, but the degree is smaller. They added that the share of memory semiconductors in Japan's exports is much smaller. The IMF projects that the South Korean economy will grow by 3.3%this year. For an industrial nation with an economy as large as Korea's, this growth rate is enormous. Furthermore, it is a figure that has already been revised upward twice since the April forecast. What is even more noteworthy here is the potential growth rate. The growth speed an economy can maintain without overstretching itself. In other words, it is the fundamental strength of the economy. South Korea's potential growth rate fell throughout the 2010s, at one point dropping to the 1%range. However, based on IMF projections, it will rise for two consecutive years, this year and next, recovering to the low 2%range . Rising for two straight years is a feat not seen in 40 years, since the so-called "three lows" boom of 1986 and 1987. On the other hand, Japan remains at the 0.8%level. Even when compared with G7 nations, the only country higher than South Korea is the United States. Analysts attribute this to increased investment in semiconductor facilities and improved productivity driven by AI. GDP per capita will approach the $ 50,000 mark by next year . Calculating with IMF figures, among economies with a population of over 20 million, it also surpasses Taiwan and Italy. Conversely, Japan, which once challenged the U.S., is expected to fall to 6th in the world next year, trailing India and the U.K. in total economic size. South Korea's GDP per capita first surpassed Japan's in 2023, and this year, the gap is widening significantly. What on earth has happened? Let's turn the clock back. As recently as 1960, South Korea and Taiwan were equally poor. At that time, Japan's GDP per capita was already three times that of South Korea. Then, in 1965, the Korea-Japan Treaty was signed. The claims funds received at that time were used to build POSCO. In 1970, the Gyeongbu Expressway was opened. If you watch videos from when it opened, there were almost no cars on this wide road. Meanwhile, Japan was hosting Asia's first Expo in Osaka that same year. And in 1973, Japan's GDP per capita was 10 times that of Korea's. In terms of multiples, this was when the gap was the widest. The overall economic scale was more than 30 times larger. But after that, Korea started catching up rapidly. In 1994, it surpassed $ 10,000 in GDP per capita for the first time. However, as the yen's value soared the following year, Japan's GDP per capita rose to $ 45,000 . Japan had already surpassed $ 40,000 more than 30 years ago. The gap in GDP per capita between Korea and Japan was a staggering $ 32,000. In terms of dollar amount, the gap was at its largest then. No matter how much we chased, Japan was always far ahead. The trend shifted as we entered the 2010s. Japan's GDP per capita, converted into dollars, peaked in 2012. After that, as the yen's value halved, Japan's GDP per capita in dollar terms continued to shrink. And finally, in 2023, Korea surpassed Japan. You might ask if we only won because of the weak yen. If you only look at the final moment of reversal, that is true. If the exchange rate had remained as it was in 2012, Japan would still be ahead. However, narrowing a ten-fold gap to within two-fold was entirely due to our own strength. If you enjoyed this video so far, please like and subscribe. As the won's value fell last year, we became nearly equal to Japan, and Taiwan surpassed us again. The IMF projects that Taiwan will be slightly ahead this year, but Korea will overtake them again next year. It’s a case of two semiconductor powerhouses neck-and-neck . But there is a particularly notable point in this year's forecast. It shows GDP per capita jumping by over $ 7,000 in just one year. What on earth could have happened in just one year? The core is exports. Specifically, semiconductors. Last September, monthly exports exceeded $ 120 billion for the first time in history, and semiconductors accounted for half of that. The IMF also noted that Korea and Taiwan hold an overwhelming share in AI computer equipment. But the factor that had a greater impact than volume was price. I mentioned earlier that this year's growth rate is just over 3%. The production volume increased by that much, but we sold them at a very high price. The export price of DRAM in September rose more than three times in a year. This means that selling at high prices had a much greater effect than just selling more. As a result, posts like this have appeared on the Japanese internet. What is the point of a GDP inflated by TSMC, Samsung, and SK Hynix ? That is not entirely wrong. However, money earned from selling goods at high prices is still clearly real income. The real issues are who receives that money and how long this boom will last. Let's look at three tasks we need to address moving forward. First, they say the national economy has improved, but what about our own wallets? In the second quarter, profits for businesses and self-employed individuals grew by nearly 50%year-on-year. In contrast, the total wages received by workers during the same period only grew by 8%. In fact, real per-capita wages, adjusted for inflation, fell for four consecutive months from April to July. Employees in the semiconductor divisions of Samsung and Hynix are expected to receive average performance bonuses in the hundreds of millions of won this year. However, workplaces that share profits with employees like this account for only about six out of every hundred. This is why there is such a disconnect between claims that the economy is doing well and the feeling that our personal lives have improved. Second, what happens if the semiconductor craze cools down? Right now, memory prices continue to rise, and Samsung Electronics exceeded 100 trillion won in quarterly operating profit for the first time in the third quarter. However, the KOSPI has fallen nearly 40%from its peak this summer. This is due to growing doubts about whether the massive investments poured into AI will be recovered on time. A bigger problem is that the national budget itself relies heavily on semiconductors. Next year's budget exceeds 820 trillion won, and it is supported by tax revenue that has surged due to the semiconductor boom. If the semiconductor market cools, tax revenue could drop in one or two years, but government spending is difficult to cut once it has been increased. The rebound in the potential growth rate is a similar case. Some point out that the figures rose only because of the semiconductor boom, and there is no clear evidence that productivity has improved excluding the sector. Third, it is the population problem. Korea's total fertility rate once dropped to the lowest level in the world. Fortunately, the number of births has increased every month for over two years, and the total fertility rate in July rose from a year ago to reach 0.89 . However, while 1 million babies were born in 1970, that number was just 250,000 last year. The working-age population is projected to be less than half of what it is now in 50 years. This is precisely why the driver for the recent rise in the potential growth rate was equipment and technology, not labor. According to UN projections, Korea will surpass Japan in the proportion of its elderly population by the mid-2040s. This means the next area where we might overtake Japan is the aging of our society. Let's go back to that empty Gyeongbu Expressway in 1970. It is nothing short of a miracle that a country whose per capita GDP was one-tenth of Japan's has now surpassed it. It is all thanks to the people who paved these roads, built factories, and worked tirelessly. According to the IMF's outlook, we are on the verge of reaching a per capita GDP of $ 50,000 next year. However, the passing lane was never meant for staying in for long . Once the passing is done, what we need now is the skill to keep running out in front. What are your thoughts on this?
