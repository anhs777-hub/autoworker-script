Chanel doubled its bag prices and still raked in 2 trillion won in Korea, while Hermès saw its sales jump by over 70%. Department store luxury sections are still booming. However, right next to those glittering lights, there are luxury brands that have seen their sales cut in half over three years, packing their bags as if they were being kicked out of the department store. Even Dior, which stirred up the whole country with its 80,000 won production cost, is just the least damaged among the fallen when you line them up. Six other massive names have sunk even deeper to the bottom than Dior. It’s not that people stopped buying them because they were expensive . The reason these seven names collapsed lies somewhere entirely different. We’ll trace these items— which you might have at home or have seen countless times on the street— starting from the 7th place, to see why they became names no one looks at overnight. 7th place is Dior. If you have ever prepared for a wedding, the topic of a "wedding gift bag" surely came up. It’s the luxury bag that a bride receives as a wedding gift. At one point, the regular in that spot was Dior's Lady Dior. It’s the very bag that Ye-seo's mom carried in different colors in the drama*Sky Castle*. It was nicknamed the "wedding gift bag" for a reason. In 2019 alone, Dior raised prices in Korea four times, and this bag rose by nearly 10%that October, yet it still sold like hotcakes. When COVID -19 hit and Chanel bags became scarce, people lined up in front of Dior stores instead. In January 2022, something like this even happened. A 31-year-old bride-to-be, ahead of her May wedding, paid for a bag in full and waited for it to arrive, but the store called her right before a price hike. They asked her to cancel the order because there were no plans for the item to arrive. Around that time, the price of that bag went from 6.5 million won to 7.6 million won. People lashed out, asking if Korean consumers were being treated as pushovers, but sales did not falter. Dior’s Korean revenue, which was 63.7 billion won in 2017, reached 1.0456 trillion won in 2023. It jumped more than 16 times in just six years. But then, in June of the year before last, that famous news broke. It was that Dior bags costing over 3 million won were being made in Italian subcontract factories for about 80,000 won in our money. Customers were furious, asking if they had been white-gloving and authenticating 80,000 won bags at the store all that time. At this point, you might all be thinking this. Dior must have collapsed because of these 80,000 won. But it’s difficult to view it only that way. Less than a month after the news broke, an official from a luxury platform said that Dior's sales volume hadn't actually decreased. There were also customers who said that regardless of production costs, luxury is about the brand value. Then where did all those long lines go? The summer before last, a customer in her 30s said this. She said she had already acquired the basics like Chanel, Hermes, and Dior, and now she wanted to get into jewelry. In fact, in the first quarter of this year at a major department store in Seoul, luxury goods sales grew by nearly 30%, but jewelry sales jumped by over 55%. A bridal gift bag is similar to a wedding ring. Once you get one, there's rarely a need to go buy another. Since people bought all at once during the pandemic the bags they would have bought over several years, there was no demand left afterward. Dior's Korean sales dropped from their peak in 2023 to 773.9 billion won last year, a decline of over 270 billion won in two years. If you value one Lady Dior at 8.95 million won, that’s over 30,000 fewer bags sold. Still, compared to 2022, which is the benchmark for rankings, it dropped 16.8%, the least among the seven brands. That’s because it went up once more the year after 2022 before coming back down. Even so, it is still a giant that sells 700 billion won worth of goods a year. That Dior joined other luxury brands this month at a department store's wedding fair. It’s a brand that rarely offers extra benefits, yet it joined a department store event that doubles mileage points for couples-to-be. Four years ago, it was a brand that would take payment and then turn away a bride-to-be's order. The spot for bridal gifts is still there. However, these days, rings and necklaces are filling that spot more often than bags. From 6th place, there are no giants like Dior. The next one is a luxury brand that once even lowered prices for Korean customers. 6th place is the Italian brand Tod's. You’ve probably seen those flat leather shoes with rubber pebbles on the sole at least once. They are driving shoes made to make pressing the pedals more comfortable while driving. Tod's is the place that made its name with this one shoe. When Tod's first opened at a department store in Apgujeong, Seoul, in 2004, it was called the last luxury brand not yet introduced to Korea. You had to wait over two months to buy one popular bag. A few years later, it entered the top 10 in luxury sales at that department store and was called a "new luxury brand that only you and I know." Then, the customer base expanded. A 41- year-old office worker choosing 600- dollar Tod's shoes at an airport duty-free shop appeared in the newspaper, and sales rose after Kim Woo-bin wore red and blue driving shoes in the drama "The Heirs." By 2019, people were even saying that Tod's was the one that turned driving shoes from a luxury for the rich into footwear for every driver. There is one more scene regarding this brand that Korean customers might remember. They once lowered their prices. As the Korean won strengthened in 2006, they cut prices by 30%, and their iconic Gommini loafers, which cost 619,000 won in 2008 , dropped to 495,000 won by 2011. At the time, the head of the Korean branch stated that they lowered prices to match the exchange rate. That was even as Chanel raised its prices five times during the same period. However, Tod's has raised its prices since then, and today, their Gommini loafers are in the 900,000 won range. Since you think of shoes when you hear "Tod's," you’d naturally assume shoes are still their best-selling items. But this summer, the CEO of Tod's Korea mentioned in an interview that bags and clothes are selling much better than shoes these days. Coming from a company that made its name with driving shoes. Their sales in Korea fell from 54.8 billion won in 2022 to 44.8 billion won last year, a drop of 18.3%. It’s as if a store that used to sell five pairs is now selling just over four. An earnings report last year analyzed that while prices rose, the volume of sales decreased significantly. Meanwhile, the standards for choosing footwear were also shifting. In the first quarter of this year, one department store saw sales of comfortable casual shoes jump 30%, and we even heard news of more children giving sneakers instead of carnations for Parents 'Day. At the higher end, a different door opened. Back in 2022, there were already reports that you could buy Hermès loafers for around 1.4 million won. A retail executive said that, in extreme terms, only cost-effective products or the most expensive luxury items are selling this year. Loafers in the 900,000 won range are stuck right in the middle. On the secondhand market, a Tod's D-Bag, which cost 1,965,000 won in 2011, is now listed for around 200,000 won. The bag that was once considered a top choice for a wedding gift for a stylish mother-in-law is now worth one-tenth of its original price. Tod's was loved because it was the most comfortable shoe among dress shoes. But these days, with even gifts for parents shifting to sneakers, no dress shoe can beat a sneaker in terms of comfort. The most comfortable dress shoe, originally made to make hitting the pedals easier, has become just a slightly more comfortable dress shoe when placed next to a sneaker. At least Tod's shoes were pristine, new shoes to begin with. This brand makes them look like old shoes from the start. This brand is Golden Goose. A brand new pair of sneakers with scuffed toes and dark, worn-out heels. Have you ever seen them on your child or nephew's feet? They aren't scratches. They were intentionally made to look worn out at the factory. Those sneakers with the star-shaped leather patch on the side are Golden Goose. In 2014, these sneakers gained fame through word-of-mouth as "celebrity sneakers." The entire supply imported to Korea sold out, and prices over 500,000 to 600,000 won were considered cheap. The following year, sales at one online shopping mall surged more than tenfold in just one year. In June 2017, then-Hyundai Motor Vice Chairman Chung Eui-sun stepped onto the stage for a new car launch wearing these sneakers. Newspapers wrote that anyone who knew a thing or two about fashion had at least a pair or two. Since then, these sneakers have gradually trickled down the social ladder. That autumn, children’s sizes were released for moms and kids to wear matching shoes, and by the next year, they became couple sneakers for drama characters. In 2020, they were dubbed a luxury item for teens that drains parents' pockets, and by 2022, they made the list of " bully fashion" among students. And the year before last, a story about a 38- year-old mom in Dongtan who bought 380,000-won Golden Goose sneakers for her 18-month-old daughter appeared in a British newspaper. It took seven years to go from the feet of a conglomerate vice chairman to the feet of a toddler learning to walk. At this point, you might think the company itself, Golden Goose, must be wobbling. But that’s not the case. The headquarters’ revenue has seen double-digit growth for three consecutive years. They are still selling well globally. What fell was Korea. The year with the highest sales in Korea was 2022, at 49.1 billion won. But that very autumn. A department store official said the brand had developed a somewhat "old" image among those in their 20s to 40s. In the year they sold the most, they were already being called a worn-out name. Last year’s sales in Korea were 35.8 billion won; 27%vanished in just three years. It means that for every four pairs they used to sell, more than one pair is now gone. In reporting its first-quarter results this year, the head office noted they intentionally reduced online retailers in Korea. The asking price for these sneakers currently on the secondhand market is around 100,000 won. That’s about one-fourth of the retail price. In February the year before last, something like this also happened. A customer who took their 740,000-won white Golden Goose shoes to a dry cleaner got them back stained with yellowish dirt. When they demanded compensation, the dry cleaner refused. They argued that these luxury sneakers could lose color if washed, and more importantly, they were dirty shoes to begin with. It’s easy to understand if you think of ripped jeans. When only a few trendsetters wear them, it’s style, but when everyone in town starts wearing them, they just look like tattered pants. Even intentional scratches only carry value when not everyone can have them. Once everyone and their neighbor starts wearing them. What was 700,000 won worth of "cool" becomes just "dirt" at the dry cleaner. Up to this point, these three places still sell seven or eight out of every ten items they used to. From fourth place on, more than four out of ten have disappeared. The first one is a luxury brand that Koreans started laughing at. Fourth place is Valentino. This past January, a single luxury vest turned the Korean internet into a sea of ridicule. It was a 6.3 million won vest from Valentino, but it looked just like the grandmother’s vests you can buy at the market for 5,000 won. Comments flooded in saying it’s too expensive, so let’s just wear the original. A month later, broadcaster Choi A-jeong bought two vests at Gyeongdong Market for 17,000 won and said this. "See? It's Valentino.""Is it because I don't have money that I can't buy Valentino?" Just a few years ago, no one would have laughed at this name. What made Valentino famous in Korea was the 'Rockstud,' a small, pyramid-shaped metal stud. Thanks to these studs lined up on shoe toes and bag edges, you could tell it was Valentino from afar. In 2016, a department store in Gangnam, Seoul, opened the country's largest shoe section, giving Valentino its first standalone shoe store alongside Louis Vuitton, Gucci, and Ferragamo. Even their 690,000 won sneakers caused a sell-out frenzy. But those studs started appearing in more and more places. In 2018, a TV home shopping channel received 1,720 orders for the Rockstud series over four broadcasts. In 2020, Rockstud bags piled up in duty-free shops appeared with 38%off tags, and an article was published about an 18-year-old student who worked part-time at a barbecue restaurant on weekends to wear 700,000 won Valentino sneakers. In 2022, Valentino sneakers made it onto the list of "bully fashion " among students, and in the drama "The Glory," school bully Park Yeon-jin carried a 2.4 million won Rockstud clutch. A 35-year-old office worker said that no matter how expensive brands labeled as "bully fashion" are, their domestic image is so fixed that she doesn't want to buy them. The studs were, so to speak, a nametag that told everyone it was Valentino from a distance. But the meaning of a nametag changes depending on who is wearing it. A nametag worn by a Gangnam department store customer is not the same as one worn by a bully in a drama. So, are they not buying it because it's expensive? It was actually the opposite . Department store seasonal sales offered up to 40%off repeatedly, and some in their 20s claimed they bought used bags for 500,000 won that cost over 2 million won new. Despite more ways to buy it cheaply, Valentino Korea's revenue dropped 40.3%from 61.3 billion won in 2022 to 36.6 billion won last year. It’s as if for every five pairs a store used to sell, two have disappeared. They even packed up and left a Myeong-dong duty-free shop in Seoul, which was their first city duty-free store back in 2018, the autumn before last. And just last month , Valentino reopened its doors in that very same duty-free shop. They put forward shoes that were a new interpretation of the Rockstud line. Studded shoes hanging in the Myeong-dong duty-free shop once again. Whose nametag will they be seen as this time? Until Valentino, one single item used to carry the weight of the entire brand name. Third place goes to a name that one person staked their life on. Third place is Etro. If you open your mother’s closet drawer, you might find a scarf with a teardrop-shaped pattern that curves slightly at the end . That pattern is called paisley. Etro is the brand that brought this pattern to the necks of Korean mothers. And behind it all, there was one person. In the early 1990s, Lee Choong-hee, a former Hotel Shilla duty-free executive , took his entire savings of 8 million won and went to visit the Etro chairman in Japan. He carried a business plan that was hundreds of pages long in his hands. After persistent visits, he secured the Korean distribution rights, but within a year, headquarters demanded he open department store locations as well. So, he even put his own apartment up for sale. He recalled that his wife was deeply shocked by the decision. That gamble paid off. In the first half of 2000, sales jumped 70% from the previous year, and the customer base grew younger, shifting from those in their 40s and 50s to those in their 20s and 30s. Back then, paisley was a youthful pattern. In 2006 , it became the first luxury brand to be officially sold on TV home shopping, selling out every single broadcast. The term "home shopping Louis Vuitton" emerged, and word-of-mouth helped department store sales rise by 10%to 20 %as well. The Boston bag, which sold over 10,000 units on home shopping alone, earned the nickname "7-second bag" because you’d see one on the street every seven seconds. It sold as a scarf for mothers during Lunar New Year and as a wedding essential for newlyweds during the marriage season. However, a bag you see every seven seconds is also a bag you run into someone else wearing every seven seconds. In 2011, a newspaper reporter met with a designer from the Etro family and asked bluntly. Since Etro is known as a middle-aged brand in Korea, are there any plans to transform it into a young luxury brand? A pattern that was once youthful had become a " mom’s pattern" in just over a decade. In 2021, Etro headquarters ended its nearly 30-year partnership with Lee Choong-hee’s company and began direct sales in Korea. You’d think the headquarters would keep more profit by cutting out the middleman, right? The results were the opposite. From the first year the headquarters took over until last year, the Korean subsidiary recorded operating losses for five consecutive years. Industry experts believed that by the time they entered the market directly, the peak had long since passed. Etro’s golden age in Korea was the early 2000s, during that one person's era. Starting in the fall of 2022, a new designer began to move paisley out of its spotlight role in clothing. The head of Etro's Korean subsidiary admitted that it was true that Etro was seen as somewhat stagnant and old-fashioned. As the pattern receded, people stopped saying it was a "brand that moms carry." But, unfortunately, the sales receded along with it. 18.8 billion won in 2022, then 16.3 billion won, then 12.6 billion won , and last year, 11.2 billion won. Over three years. 7%has disappeared. In terms of a shop, it's like the headquarters taking over a store that an individual had built up, even by mortgaging their own home. But after taking it over, they realized that the regular customers who used to crowd that shop had already become mothers. It took one man’s 8 million won to bring Etro to Korea. The brand in second place is a name that a woman flew all the way to Italy to protect in front of the chairman. That brand is Ferragamo. Many of you probably remember buying a necktie for your father for the holidays. Ferragamo was once a necktie that frequently appeared in articles about holiday gifts. There must have been homes where your mother had at least one pair of Ferragamo shoes with a ribbon in her shoe rack. There was one person’s negotiation behind this brand establishing itself in Korean department stores. In 1994, 42-year-old Jang Myung-sook sat down with the Ferragamo chairman in Italy. She is someone better known today as the YouTuber "Milan Donna." In her own words, a Seoul department store paid a large sum to obtain the exclusive rights, but Ferragamo insisted they couldn't work with them, claiming there were no buyers who could select the right products. After declining several times because her oldest child was only 93 days old, she finally stepped up at the department store's request. And she told the chairman. To trust her and continue working with this department store. That department store was Sampoong Department Store. In June of that year, the department store collapsed. Jang Myung-sook said she also lost colleagues in that accident where many people lost their lives. Afterward, Ferragamo asked her to take over their Korean sales, but she refused, and Ferragamo entered Korea by establishing a local subsidiary through another path. Ferragamo, which entered in that way, became the style of a generation. The Vara shoes with the ribbon ornament were a must-have for the "Cheongdam-dong daughter-in-law" look in the early 2000s, and Ferragamo loafers were a bestseller among middle-aged men. In 2007, it rose to second place in luxury goods sales at one department store. But right around that time, the way to work began to change. In 2008, as Samsung Group switched to casual dress codes, more and more companies started letting employees ditch their ties. A few years later, it was said that necktie production had dropped to one-tenth of what it was 10 years prior. By the 2013 Chuseok holiday, Ferragamo ties, belts, and wallets were even appearing on convenience store gift lists, and the following year, articles reported that you could buy Ferragamo ties at European outlets for around 50,000 won. In March 2014, it pulled its store out of the Galleria Luxury Hall in Cheongdam-dong, Seoul, after nine years . It was the first case of Ferragamo, once called one of the top five luxury brands, being pushed out of a major Seoul department store. Then, in 2022, the luxury boom lifted Ferragamo back up. Sales in Korea reached 140.4 billion won, the highest in the last five years. You probably thought they had finally made a comeback. But the very next year, it dropped to 98.4 billion won, falling nearly 30%in just one year. The Italian headquarters even specifically singled out Korea in their semi-annual report as the reason for the decline in Asian sales. Last year, it was 82.8 billion won. In just three years, a significant percentage has vanished. Come to think of it, Ferragamo ties were items you bought as gifts for your father rather than for yourself. Gifts are exchanged only when the recipient has a use for them. When fathers loosened their ties on their way to work, the reason to buy them ties vanished as well. Last January, at a department store in Daegu, Ferragamo packed up and left along with four other luxury brands. The department store filled the vacant spaces with the mid-priced clothing brand Top Ten, the shoe store ABC Mart, and the cosmetics store Olive Young. Thirty years ago, these luxury brands refused to supply goods to Korean department stores. Now, sneaker shops are moving into the department stores that those luxury brands abandoned. Ferragamo has slipped and recovered several times over the last 30 years. The top spot is different. It actually started to slide from the very year it reached its peak. The top spot belongs to Fendi. If you watched the drama "Now, We Are Breaking Up," you probably remember the bag Song Hye-kyo carried. It’s Fendi’s Peekaboo, a bag that reveals its interior when you open the flap. By the time the drama ended, the bag was outright nicknamed the "Song Hye-kyo bag." In fact, Fendi hadn't been a leading brand for a long time. In 2012, a CEO of a luxury resale shop identified Fendi as a brand favored by people in their 50s and 60s. But riding the wave of "revenge spending" during the pandemic, Fendi's sales jumped 27.5 %in 2020, and the face of that trend was the Song Hye-kyo bag. Sales in 2022 were 116.6 billion won. The following year, it reached 152.2 billion won, the highest sales ever. And in February 2023, Fendi opened a four-story building in Cheongdam-dong, Seoul. Modeled after their headquarters in Rome, they built a 16-meter-high arch on the front, wrapped in LED lights. The name is Palazzo Fendi Seoul. In Korean, that’s "Fendi Palace." Song Hye-kyo, Lee Min-ho, and Kim Da-mi attended the opening ceremony. Since they opened a palace during their peak sales year, you’d assume they earned the most money, right? That year, Fendi Korea recorded an operating loss of 8.9 billion won. A year earlier, they had a profit of 9.2 billion won. Meanwhile, the price of the Song Hye-kyo bag kept rising without pause. In 2022, they raised prices twice, in February and October, and by October, the Peekaboo Mini Selleria went from 5.7 million won to 6.04 million won. The following October, the Peekaboo and Baguette bags rose by about 10%, and then they went up again in January and April. As luxury prices kept rising, customers complained that prices were going up without any real changes in quality or design. An industry insider noted that while Fendi did well after the pandemic , it isn't a luxury brand with a particularly deep fan base. In the first half of the year before last, Fendi’s department store sales dropped by 26%. From January to July of last year, while luxury fashion sales at one department store grew by 5%, jewelry and watch sales jumped by 33%. It means that while bags are easily replaced, jewelry and watches last a long time. Fendi Korea's sales last year were 87.7 billion won, marking a 42.1%decline over three years. In 2022, the company sold over 400 million won worth of goods a day, but last year that figure fell to 240 million won. Then, on August 17th of last year, Fendi closed its store at a department store in Gwanggyo, Gyeonggi Province. It was a location they had entered as a pop-up store five years ago when the department store first opened. The department store stated the contract ended by mutual agreement, and Moncler was set to take that spot. You know how items bought in the excitement of a trip often end up in a drawer once you get home, right? Perhaps what we bought from Fendi during the revenge spending spree was more about that year's excitement than the bags themselves. That excitement faded in a season, but the four-story palace in Cheongdam-dong was built when that feeling was at its peak. Just two and a half years after opening that palace, Fendi vacated its spot in the department store. I looked into all seven names. Though the items and customers were all different, the moments when customers lost interest overlapped in three ways. First, when the season for them ended. This was the case for the bag seen in a drama on a character's mom, the Song Hye-kyo bag, and celebrity sneakers. Names that rode the trend of the time came back down as soon as that wind died down. Second, when the type of person carrying the item changed. Sneakers that moved from a vice chairman’s feet to a baby’s, and studded items that moved from luxury department store shoe sections to students and drama villains. This was true for the brand that went from being for young women to being for moms . Third, when they became too easy to buy. Bags sold by the thousands on home shopping, ties appearing on convenience store Chuseok gift lists, and studded luxury goods sold on TV with duty-free stock discounts. If you run into even two friends at a reunion carrying the same bag as you, that bag rarely makes it to the next gathering. Luxury goods were the same way. Of course, you might ask if the controversy over 80,000 won production costs or the annual price hikes are also to blame. I also believe that impact was significant. However, there are numbers that cannot be explained by feeling alone. During the same three years, department store luxury sales actually increased. Loro Piana, famous for cashmere, even faced Italian court oversight due to labor issues, yet their Korean sales rose by 16.9%last year. Chanel more than doubled the price of its classic bag from 7.15 million won in 2019 to over 16 million won last year, and their Korean sales exceeded 2 trillion won for the first time that year. After following all seven locations, I have one answer. These brands didn't collapse because they were expensive. They collapsed because they became too common. And customers 'money has moved toward things not everyone can possess yet. In the same three years, Hermès' Korean sales grew by 73%, and in department stores, jewelry and watches are selling much faster than bags. There are also names outside the list that couldn't even make it onto this ranking. It’s Gucci. By changing its corporate structure twice within two months in 2020, it became a company that no longer has to disclose its Korean sales in audit reports. Meanwhile, Gucci's global sales dropped by over 40%compared to 2022, and half a year after Fendi left the Gwanggyo department store, Gucci also gave notice of its withdrawal. So, check these three things to see if that luxury item in your closet or the one you plan to buy next is still solid. One, does the brand remind you of just one drama or a single product? Two, have the people carrying it lately shifted to high schoolers, babies, or your parents 'generation? Three, does the name keep appearing in home shopping, outlets, or second-hand markets? If none apply, you can rest easy. If one applies, it's worth watching, and if two apply, customers' eyes are already moving toward other signs. If all three apply, you might find that name on a list like this again next year. At the end of the list , Dior reopened its department store branch in the heart of Seoul last August, expanding it into a two-story space connecting the first and second floors. This month, they also participated in a wedding fair. Whether the lines that were long enough to turn away brides-to-be will form again will likely be revealed by the audit report coming out next spring.
