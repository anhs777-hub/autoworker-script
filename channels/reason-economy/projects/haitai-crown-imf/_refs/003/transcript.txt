There was once a massive empire that climbed to become the 24th largest conglomerate in Korea. You could find the snacks and ice cream made by this company anywhere in the country, and they reached the top of professional baseball nine times over 20 seasons. Even though they were over 3 trillion won in debt, financial institutions scrambled to lend them money. The problem was that this money wasn't being used to make snacks. They built electronics factories, jumped into heavy industry, and even reached into the construction business. Why would a snack company do such a thing? Let’s dig into how the four characters of " Haitai Group" once ruled South Korea and then disappeared into history. The story goes back to Seoul right after the Japanese withdrew. There was a small snack factory run by a Japanese man in Namyeong-dong, Yongsan. It was called Nagaoka Confectionery, and there was one Korean youth who handled the accounting there. His name was Park Byung-gyu. He was the only Korean working in an office position among the Japanese. When liberation came, the Japanese owner hastily packed his bags and left for his home country. The factory was left empty, with only the machines remaining. An ordinary person would have been worried about where to hide during such chaotic times. But this young man was different. He teamed up with three colleagues he worked with and took over the entire factory. They had taken over a former enemy-owned enterprise. The four of them joined forces to start a company, and while thinking about a name, they thought of the mythical creature, the Haitai. It meant they would do business honestly, just like the creature that judges right from wrong and upholds justice. The problem was, who would buy snacks when there was nothing to eat? Because the entire country was starving. So the first product these people chose was sweet red bean jelly, or yanggaeng. It was a traditional snack made by sweetening red beans, and it received an unexpectedly good response. In times of hunger, where else would you find something sweet? It felt like you gained energy after just one bite. It is said that they made it by boiling agar and red beans themselves in a cauldron. As word spread, it began to sell little by little. But before they could even get settled, war broke out. The factory was destroyed by bombing, and the employees were scattered everywhere. More than half of them lost their lives during the war. For a normal company, this would have been the end. But the surviving employees grabbed a small pot and set off on a path of refuge. Somewhere in Busan, they set up a tent and boiled red bean jelly in that small pot. Even in the midst of war, there were people looking for something sweet. In this way, Haitai set a legendary record of never stopping production for a single day since its founding. After the war, Haitai returned to Seoul and began to spread its wings in earnest. They got ideas while looking at the gum and candy that flowed out of US military bases at the time. Couldn't we make something like that, too? Back then, all chewing gum was imported. We decided to try making it ourselves and brought in machines from Japan. That’s how domestic gum was born. But there was a pretty absurd incident here . The biggest hit among Haitai’s gum at the time was "Cigar Gum," shaped exactly like a cigarette. The ad slogan was, "Daddy and me, Cigar Gum." If that happened today, there’d be an uproar. But back then, it sold like crazy. Children just wanted to imitate adults and pretend to smoke. Anyway, thanks to this gum, the company completely changed. What’s even more surprising is what came next. When summer comes, what do people look for? Something cool . Back then, the only summer snacks were ice pops made of frozen sugar water, but Haitai went a step further. They decided to try making the soft ice cream they’d seen in Europe. They sent employees to Denmark to purchase the machines. But there was a problem. To make ice cream, you need skim milk powder, which didn't exist in Korea at the time. Where did they get it? From the U.S. military stationed in Korea. They sourced skim milk powder from the U.S. bases and started making ice cream . It’s said that the Haitai factory had such good hygiene standards that they even qualified to supply the U.S. military. That was the connection they had. However, there was an issue with the cone becoming soggy. No matter what they tried, they couldn't solve it. The method they found after much thought is brilliant. They coated the inside of the cone with chocolate. This prevented the cone from getting wet even when the ice cream melted. But with the technology at the time, it was difficult to coat it evenly. So, the chocolate would pool and harden at the bottom, which actually turned into a huge hit. You know how there's a chocolate chunk at the very bottom of the cone after you finish the ice cream ? People loved it and thought of it as a bonus. A mistake caused by technical limitations became a signature feature. The name of this ice cream was Bravo Cone. As soon as it launched, it was a sensation nationwide. Wholesalers lined up in front of the factory, and so many crowded in that they even had to shut the doors. Daily production would sell out in an instant. It was that remarkable. During the North-South Red Cross talks, when they gave Bravo Cones to the Northerners, they reportedly couldn't believe it was actually made in the South. They were so suspicious that it might be a U.S. product, they allegedly had to show them the company address on the wrapper to convince them . That just goes to show how high the quality was. Bravo Cone has sold over 4.8 billion units to date, with cumulative sales exceeding 1.7 trillion won. If you lined them up, they would circle the Earth 26 times. The success of Bravo Cone was just the beginning. Haitai went on to pour out one hit product after another. Matdongsan, with its sweet honey and nutty fried rice bits, was a snack you just couldn't stop eating. Over 2.8 billion bags have been sold so far; if laid end to end, it would reach the moon and back. The baseball-shaped Home Run Ball was launched with the pro baseball league and flew off the shelves during the craze. The nutty cracker Ace was a popular snack with drinks, and people lined up to buy Babanba because it tasted like real rice. The moist chocolate cake became a mega brand, raking in 4.5 billion won in monthly sales. Half of the snack section in convenience stores was filled with Haitai products. Along with Lotte Confectionery, it reigned as an undisputed powerhouse of the industry. With the company doing so well, they turned their eyes elsewhere. Pro baseball was just starting then, but there was no local team in the Jeolla region. They asked major corporations to take it on, but everyone refused. They feared losses in a region that was politically alienated. Even large Honam-based firms like the Kumho Group declined. They even went to the Blue House to ask if it was the President's wish, but since it wasn't, they backed out. When no one else would step up, Haitai raised its hand. They founded a baseball team based in Gwangju. The founder, Chairman Park Byung-kyu, was originally from Gwangju. It’s said that Lotte strongly opposed the move at first. They argued that their food industry rival, Haitai, shouldn't enter pro baseball. Honestly, everyone laughed at them in the beginning. A snack company running a baseball team? The founding roster had only 15 players . Other teams were backed by massive conglomerates, but Haitai had little funding and poor facilities. Even the players 'training grounds were inadequate. A player who joined as a batter had to pitch too, setting a record of 30 homers and 10 wins in one season. That just goes to show how short-handed they were. But do you know what happened to this team? They won the championship just two years after their founding. And it wasn't just any win—they set an unprecedented record of four consecutive championships. Winning nine titles over 20 seasons meant their win rate was nearly 50 percent. It was a record even higher than the legendary New York Yankees. What was the secret? Because they had no money, the players bonded even closer. Their fighting spirit to defend the pride of their neglected region was incredible. The fans were also wild with enthusiasm. Gwangju stadium was always packed, and the cheering was on another level from other teams. When Haitai won, the whole town felt like a festival. TV personality Kim Byung-jo, from the region, even said he would only eat Bravo Cone and always bought Oh Yes instead of Choco Pie. That shows how deep the local affection was. The company famous for snacks became a household name through baseball as well . By this point, Haitai was more than just a company. It was part of people’s everyday lives. Children held Bravo cones in their hands, and the whole family would watch Haitai Tigers games in the evening. During the holidays, gift sets of Haitai snacks were piled up in every home. There wasn't a single person in Korea who didn't know Haitai. Ranked 24th in the business world with 15 affiliates and 2.7 trillion won in revenue, a company that started with a single snack had built a massive empire. During the Seoul Olympics, the top singers of the time all appeared in a Bravo Cone commercial, so you can imagine just how great its status was. The problem was that this success fueled one person's ambition. The founder, Chairman Park Byung-kyu, passed away suddenly at the young age of 52. It was lung cancer. His 29-year-old son inherited the management rights, but his vision was completely different from his father's. An extraordinary general meeting of shareholders was held just 18 days after the founder passed away. The results were shocking. The 29-year-old son, who had been a section chief in planning, was suddenly promoted to managing director. The next year he became an executive director, and the year after that, he became a vice president. Then, at age 33, he took the seat of chairman leading the entire Haitai Group. He was a young, ambitious man who had studied in the U.S. The young man who inherited the empire his father had built over 30 years was named Park Kun-bae. He had completely different ideas from his father. There was something he said as soon as he took office. He would reduce the weight of food and focus on electronics and construction. He meant that the era of making big money by selling snacks, ice cream, and drinks was over. At the time , Lotte was growing, making the competition fiercer. He wanted to play in a bigger league. The problem was that while he knew snacks well, he knew nothing about electronics or construction. Even so, he did not stop. In fact, he picked up the pace even more. This is where the tragedy of Haitai begins. His first target was the electronics industry. He acquired a company called Shinbang Electronics and changed its name to Haitai Electronics. A company that used to make snacks suddenly stepped up to make electronic products. They lacked technical prowess and the competition was intense. Major companies like Samsung and LG were already dominating the market. Even so, he didn't stop the investment. He acquired an audio specialty company called Inkel. It was the leading domestic audio company at the time. Next, he even bought a cordless phone company called Now Precision. He also jumped into the cable TV equipment business. A snack company was making audio equipment and telephones. It doesn't make any sense from a common-sense perspective. That wasn't the end of it. He even entered the construction business. They acquired a company called Mijin Metal Industry and renamed it Haitai Heavy Industries. They had expanded into the heavy industry sector. They even built railway vehicles. Would you believe it if I said Haitai Heavy Industries manufactured the Mugunghwa passenger cars? A snack company was making trains . No one could understand what they were trying to do. It was a classic case of octopus-style expansion. They were recklessly stepping into fields they knew nothing about. The problem was where they were getting the money for all this expansion. Haitai Confectionery was a business with daily cash inflows. Money came in every time snacks were sold at supermarkets and convenience stores nationwide. Their cash flow was excellent. Financial institutions saw this cash flow and scrambled to lend them money. A staggering 29 merchant banking corporations offered loans to Haitai. Haitai took all that money and poured it into deficit-ridden businesses unrelated to food. They used the money earned from selling snacks to cover losses in electronics and pay off debts in heavy industry. It was like pouring water into a bottomless pit. The debt snowballed. On the surface, it looked like a glamorous conglomerate, but in reality, it was a sandcastle built on a pile of debt worth nearly 3 trillion won. To be exact, it was 2.978 trillion won. For Haitai Confectionery to pay this back, they would have had to work for decades. Yet, the expansion continued. The chairman placed bigger bets, like someone addicted to gambling . "It'll work this time.""It'll succeed this time." But there was no way they could succeed in electronics and heavy industry with snack-making skills. What was even stranger was the chairman's behavior. Even with the company in such peril, he was more focused on external activities. He served as vice president of the Boy Scouts of Korea, then rose to president. He even served as a board member of the World Organization of the Scout Movement. He also served as president of the Korea Weightlifting Federation. He put company management on the back burner and paid more attention to these honorary positions. He called it an "autonomous management system" and left each subsidiary to fend for itself. So, who was going to take responsibility? As deficits piled up and debt grew, there was no proper control tower in place. You can see what the company's culture was like by looking at how the Haitai Tigers were run. The baseball team won more championships than any other in professional baseball history. Yet, the treatment of the players was abysmal. They were a notoriously stingy club. Do you know what the bonus was even after winning a championship? It was a Haitai Confectionery gift set. They gave them snacks instead of cash. There’s a legendary story that they received a snack set worth less than 10,000 won for winning the championship ring. Player salaries were also much lower compared to other clubs. That's why a famous incident broke out. It was the season after their championship. The club actually said they would cut operating costs. They downgraded our accommodations and reduced meal budgets . They even slashed the hitting bonuses they gave to players in half. They used to pay 10,000 won for a single and 20,000 won for a double, but they cut that, too. Angered, the players decided to stage a mutiny. It happened at a dinner party hosted by the club owner. Bulgogi was served at the restaurant, but no one touched it. Nobody even picked up their chopsticks. The meat burned black until it turned into charcoal, and only smoke rose up. It was a silent protest by the players. This is what we call the "Bulgogi Mutiny" incident. The owner stormed out in a rage, and the manager flew into a fury, demanding to know who the ringleader was. Eventually, one player was branded the mastermind, fined, and suspended. It was later revealed that he wasn't actually the ringleader. The real instigators were others, but he lost the "guessing game" and unfairly took the fall. Even after this incident , the club’s stingy ways didn't change. It reached the point where other clubs used Haetae players' salaries as an example during their own negotiations. They’d say, "Even Haetae players only get this much, so why do you want more?" using it as leverage. Where did all the money earned by squeezing the baseball team go? It went toward covering the electronics company’s losses and paying off the heavy industry company’s debts. The "golden goose" known as Haetae Confectionery was laying golden eggs daily, but those eggs were all being spent elsewhere. Their snacks were still selling well. Matdongsan, Homerun Ball, and Bravo Cone all had strong sales. The problem was that the money wasn't being reinvested into the snack business. Instead of growing their core business, they poured money into unrelated ventures. By buying Haetae snacks, people were unknowingly covering the losses of Haetae Electronics and Haetae Heavy Industries. At the time, Haetae Group had one of the highest debt-to-equity ratios in the business world. Outwardly, it was a massive conglomerate with over 2 trillion won in sales, but it was rotting from within. Yet, the expansion didn't stop. Why was that? There was a saying that " a large enterprise is too big to fail." It meant that if a company grew large enough, it wouldn't go bankrupt. There was a belief that because tens of thousands would lose their jobs if the company fell, the government would save them somehow. The financial sector felt the same way. "There's no way Haetae will go under.""Even if things get tough, someone will bail them out." That is why they kept lending them money. But that belief would soon be shattered. A massive storm sweeping across all of Asia was approaching. The foreign exchange crisis that began in Thailand was rolling through Indonesia and heading toward Korea. That summer, a strange thing happened in Thailand. The value of the baht plummeted overnight. Foreign investors began pulling their money out all at once. Next was Indonesia. The rupiah crashed. Then came Malaysia. And that wave eventually washed over Korea. Exchange rates soared and stock prices plummeted . We had to pay back foreign loans, but we were out of dollars. Banks began to tremble. This was the very beginning of the foreign exchange crisis. In Korea, too, large corporations began to collapse one after another. Hanbo Steel was the first to fall in January. It went bankrupt with over five trillion won in debt. In March, Sammi Group fell , and in April, the Jinro Group collapsed. In July, the Kia Group, the 8th largest conglomerate, was on the brink of bankruptcy. In November, it was Haitai's turn. NewCore, Halla, and Cheonggu also collapsed together. The conglomerates were falling like dominoes. The government eventually had to reach out to the IMF. South Korea was pushed to the brink of national bankruptcy. Haitai had already been in jeopardy for months. Rumors of bankruptcy circulated in May. The company called it a lie and threatened to sue those spreading the rumors. But exactly six months later, they actually went bankrupt. In August, they faced a bankruptcy crisis after failing to clear a 20 billion won note. They barely managed to overcome it, but that was just the beginning. Cash was running dry. No matter how many snacks were sold, the revenue couldn't even cover the interest. This was because the deficits from the electronics and heavy industry divisions were too large . On the first day of November, Haitai Confectionery was officially declared bankrupt. On the same day, Haitai Electronics also went down. Daehan Packaging Industry collapsed along with them. It was 52 years after its founding. The company that started with a single small factory after liberation and rose to the 24th largest conglomerate collapsed in an instant. That Haitai, which began with Yeonyanggaeng, made Bravo Cone and Matdongsan, and led a baseball team to nine championships. The snack company that sold the most in supermarkets across the country was about to disappear into history. As the news of the bankruptcy broke, creditors flocked to the company. They were people there to collect their money. The employees stood there in a daze. Nobody knew whether they should come to work the next day or not. The factory was empty, and only the machines had stopped. Just like that factory 52 years ago, right after liberation. Back then, there was hope. Because young people jumped in, dreaming of a new era. But this time, it was different. All that was left was astronomical debt. A creditors 'group was formed. 29 merchant banks gathered to discuss countermeasures. They agreed to provide an additional 150 billion won in support. For now, the immediate fire was put out. But it was not a fundamental solution. They came up with self-rescue measures like selling real estate and stocks, but it wasn't enough to prevent bankruptcy. In the end, Haitai Confectionery filed for composition, and Haitai Electronics and Haitai Heavy Industries went into court receivership. Instead of closing the business on their own, the company was put under court management. There is a tragic story here. It’s about their relationship with Lotte. Haitai and Lotte were rivals that had competed in the confectionery industry for decades. They fought fiercely, from Choco Pie to Pepero and Pokky. But do you know what Lotte did when Haitai filed for composition? They cut off supplies. They pressured the suppliers that provided raw materials to Haitai. They said if you supply Haitai, you can't do business with Lotte. They tightened the financial lifeline. Instead of reaching out a hand when a competitor fell, they kicked them while they were down. It is said that this incident significantly worsened the public perception of Lotte in the industry. The situation kept getting worse. In June of the following year, the government made an announcement. They intended to exit 55 companies from the market. Haitai Confectionery, Haitai Corporation, and Haitai Beverage were on that list. Being an exit target meant there was no value in saving them. It meant they should be liquidated. Employees lost their jobs overnight, and suppliers collapsed in a chain reaction. Dozens of companies are connected to make just one snack. From flour and sugar to packaging and shipping, all these companies were hit hard as Haitai collapsed. The baseball team also faced a crisis. The Haitai Tigers had already finished that year's autumn season, but their future became uncertain. Since there was no money, they couldn't even pay the players' salaries on time. Ace pitcher Sun Dong-yol left for Japan. It was technically a loan, but it was effectively the same as selling him off . The team’s star hitter Lee Jong-beom also chose to head to Japan. They even transferred key players to other clubs through cash trades. A prestigious club with nine championship rings was barely surviving by selling off its players. The citizens of Gwangju were in shock. The Haitai Tigers weren't just a simple baseball team. They were the pride of the Honam region. They were a presence that helped vent the frustrations of a marginalized region. When the Tigers won at the stadium, the whole town would be in a festive mood. Such a team was now on the verge of disappearing. Fans were stomping their feet in anxiety. They pleaded to save their team. But there was no way the baseball team could be fine when the parent company had gone bankrupt. What was even more shocking was the chairman's actions. With the company in this state , there was no one taking responsibility. The chairman stepped down from the front line of management, but he faced no punishment. It’s because crimes like accounting fraud or embezzlement were not detected. They just managed the business poorly. They were incompetent. Legally, it wasn't a crime. Tens of thousands of employees, their families, and vendors were left on the streets, but no one took responsibility. This was the naked face of the chaebol system at the time. The Haitai Group entered into dissolution proceedings. Its 15 subsidiaries were scattered to the winds. Haitai Electronics shut its doors, and Haitai Heavy Industries also closed down. The division that manufactured railway vehicles was sold to another company. Haitai Corporation went bankrupt, and the owner of Haitai Distribution changed as well. The advertising agency Korad was also sold off. One by one, the name "Haitai" disappeared from them . A brand built over 50 years was being buried in history. The only thing left was Haitai Confectionery. It was their core business, making Yeonyanggaeng, Matdongsan, and Bravo Cone. The creditors decided to save at least this company. It had brand value. They are snacks known by the entire nation, after all. They converted over 840 billion won in loans into equity. They turned the debt into shares. It survived for now, but it was no longer the Haitai Confectionery of the Haitai Group. It became a company without an owner. What became of the baseball team ? Sale negotiations continued for years . Several candidates were mentioned, but none were successful. Then, Kia Motors stepped up. The Haitai Tigers changed their name to the Kia Tigers. It was fortunate for the citizens of Gwangju. Because the team didn't disappear and its home base was maintained. However, the name "Haitai" was gone forever. The Haitai logo next to the tiger mark on the uniform disappeared. The name that was with them for nine championships. What happened to the founder's son afterwards? It's said he lived a life of near-seclusion. He tried to make a comeback, but it wasn't easy. His son tried to continue the business indirectly by running a wine distribution company, but even that eventually went to another company. It was a lonely ending for someone who once led the 24th largest group in the country. But compared to what he destroyed, this was nothing. Because the lives of countless people were changed completely. The downfall of the Haitai Group showed us many things. What happens when you ignore successful businesses to dabble in unrelated ones, what kind of end you meet when you grow by debt, and what happens when an owner treats a company like private property —all those lessons were in the Haitai Group. What remained in the spot where the Haitai Group collapsed? If you follow the fates of the scattered subsidiaries one by one, interesting stories emerge. Some brands survived, and some brands vanished forever. Things that used to bear the Haitai name are now living new lives under entirely different owners. Let’s start with the story of Haitai Confectionery. A company that was barely surviving under creditor management was acquired by foreign investors. It was a consortium led by a Swiss investment firm. They created a new company called Haitai Confectionery &Foods and siphoned off only the profitable confectionery business. They left the debts behind and took only what was valuable. The remaining Haitai Confectionery changed its name, stuck to construction, and eventually shut down. But here, the owner changes once again. The Lotte Group stepped in to try and acquire it. The interesting part is Lotte’s appearance. The very same Lotte that cut off supplies when Haitai was collapsing now stepped up to acquire it. They had crushed their competitor, and now they wanted to swallow it up. But at the last minute, Crown Confectionery jumped in. A bidding war ensued, and eventually, Crown embraced Haitai. That is how the current Crown-Haitai was born. For Lotte, it was like letting it slip through their fingers at the last second. That’s why even today, when you look at the snack aisle, Lotte and Crown-Haitai products compete side-by-side. What happened to Haitai Confectionery after it was taken in by Crown? Bravo Cone, Matdongsan, and Homrun Ball are still being sold. Ace crackers are going strong, too. The brands survived. But the ironic part is the ice cream business. The division that made Bravo Cone was sold off once again. This time, Binggrae acquired it. Bravo Cone became a Binggrae product. So, if you buy a Bravo Cone now, your money goes to Binggrae, not Haitai. Even though the packaging still bears the Haitai logo. Where did Haitai Beverage go? The drink called Bong Bong . Do you remember it? That drink with the fruit bits floating inside. It was Haitai Beverage’s signature product. This company went through a Japanese firm and is now under LG Household& Health Care. The name has also changed to Haitai Htb. Coco Palm and Garabun Bae are also made here. The Haitai name remains, but the owner has completely changed. The frozen food business, famous for Kohyang Mandu, stayed with Haitai Confectionery but is now struggling against competitors like Pulmuone and CJ. Haitai Dairy went through an even more complex journey. Actually, this company had already spun off before the Haitai Group collapsed. It was taken away by the son of one of the co-founders. However, since it kept the Haitai name, it took a hit when the parent company went bankrupt. It was eventually acquired by the Dongwon Group. Today, they produce milk under the name Dongwon F&B. Do you remember the ad for that milk, saying it was high in calcium? That was originally a Haitai Dairy product. I’ll tell you the story of the baseball team next. After being acquired by Kia Motors, the Haitai Tigers became the Kia Tigers. At first, there were moves that seemed to erase the Haitai identity. Complaints erupted among the fans. Why try to wipe away the glory of the Haitai era? However, things changed as time passed. Now, they actively utilize the heritage of the Haitai days, wearing retro uniforms for games and holding events with players from the Haitai dynasty era. Because those nine championships are still part of this team's history. The Kia Tigers went on to win more titles after that. Including the Haitai era, they have reached the top a total of 12 times. It is the record for the most championships in Korean professional baseball history. Things that were unimaginable during the poor Haitai days started to happen. They offered big contracts to star players and even recruited overseas players. It was possible because the parent company was solid. It became a different level from when a confectionery company operated a baseball team. Then, was that time when a snack company ran a baseball team a mistake? Not necessarily. The reason the Haitai Tigers could sweep championships despite their circumstances was due to the players 'fighting spirit and the fans' passion. Because they lacked money, they actually stood together more firmly. Their resolve to protect the pride of the Honam region was exceptional. The loyal fan base the Kia Tigers enjoy today also has its roots in the Haitai era. The memories and affection built back then are being passed down through generations. What can we learn from the collapse of the Haitai Group? First, one must remain faithful to their core business. There was no reason for a company that succeeded in making snacks to jump into electronics and heavy industry. They poured money into fields they didn't understand well and ended up losing it all. Even now, there are companies making similar mistakes. Cases where they get distracted by this and that because things are going well, causing their core business to shake. That was exactly the case with Haitai. The second is the danger of debt. If you borrow money to expand your business, you can grow rapidly at first. But when the situation worsens, that debt becomes a shackle. Haitai was carrying nearly 3 trillion won in debt. When the economy was good, they somehow kept going, but when the financial crisis hit, they collapsed in an instant. Now, as interest rates rise, there are companies facing similar crises. The saying that history repeats itself is spot on. The third is responsible management. The chairman ruined the company, but no one was held accountable. It was the employees and partner companies who bore the pain. The person who actually made the decisions took no responsibility at all . This was a problem with the chaebol system at the time. It has improved a little now, but there is still a long way to go. The name "Haitai" carried the meaning of judging right from wrong . It is a mythical creature that protects justice, after all. That was the mindset the founders had when they established the company. To do business with integrity. But the second-generation leader who inherited that name went the complete opposite way. Reckless expansion, excessive debt , and dodging responsibility. The name "Haitai" had lost its meaning. The Haitai Group showed us what happens when you forget the spirit embedded in a company's name. If you go to a convenience store now, you can still buy Haitai snacks. The taste is almost the same, too. But the company that makes them is not the Haitai of the past. The ownership has changed time and time again. Only the name Haitai remains, while its true substance has scattered. Yet, because there are people who remember that taste, the brand continues to survive. I’m sure you’ve all had Haitai snacks when you were younger. Matdongsan from the corner store, Home Run Balls while cheering at a baseball game, or Bravo Cone in the summer; those memories have not faded. The Haitai Group may have vanished into history, but the memories of those days remain within us. I hope today’s story resonates with you in some meaningful way. A company that was with South Korea for over 50 years, starting with a single snack and leading a baseball team to a championship. It ultimately collapsed due to reckless greed, but what it left behind is still with us. The sweetness of Matdongsan, the coolness of Bravo Cone, and the Tigers 'championship flag .
