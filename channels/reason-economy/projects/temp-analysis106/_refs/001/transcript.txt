Steve Yoo won in court, yet he still couldn't return to Korea. The singer who once received cheers on stage is now in his third lawsuit over a single visa. He left as a Korean, but returned as an American. After that choice, he was distanced from the domestic stage, and even a tearful apology on his knees couldn't end the situation. However, following the news of his victory this month, the government filed an appeal. Why did the court rule in his favor, and why is the visa still not being issued? You probably remember Steve Yoo, who sang "Scissors." He was a singer who could keep singing even while performing intense choreography. Soon, "Dear Nana" and "Passion" were released. His second album reached the top of various music charts just two weeks after its release . When he left for the U.S. to prepare for an album, fans in school uniforms flocked to Gimpo Airport. Camera flashes went off at the airport, not even a concert hall. He was also frequently seen on variety shows. He showed his athleticism on "Start! Dream Team" and brought laughter on "Love Letter." He was a familiar face to families who turned on the TV on weekends. For Steve Yoo, the talk of military service began to follow him. At the time, he was a Korean with U.S. permanent residency. Having the right to live in the U.S. was different from having U.S. citizenship. In a broadcast interview, he stated his intention to accept the situation as a citizen of South Korea. He was classified for alternative public service during his physical examination . The service period reported by the news at the time was 28 months. The turning point came during his overseas schedule. Steve Yoo received permission for international travel for reasons such as performances and left the country. After finishing his concert in Japan, he moved to the U.S. and acquired citizenship there. It was January 2002. In a later interview, he explained that he had applied for citizenship two years prior due to family and overseas activities, and had tried to delay it as much as possible. Whatever the reasons behind the application were, the result revealed to fans was clear. A Korean singer set for public service had acquired U.S. citizenship and was no longer obligated to serve. The Military Manpower Administration viewed this as evasion of military duty and requested the Ministry of Justice to restrict his entry. Once he obtained a foreign nationality, the issue of his entry into Korea became a matter of a foreigner's entry. In an interview with MBC at the time, Steve Yoo apologized for failing to keep his promise and expressed his desire to continue working in Korea, contingent on being forgiven. But his plan to return to domestic activities was stopped at the airport. On the dawn of February 2nd , Steve Yoo was on a flight arriving at Incheon Airport from the U.S. A press conference was also scheduled for that day in Yeouido, Seoul. It was a schedule meant for him to personally explain his position after entering Korea. But he couldn't make it to the press conference . He was denied entry at the airport. Steve Yoo said he was embarrassed, claiming he had not anticipated being denied entry. In front of the cameras, he denied that it was a planned evasion of military service. When asked if he had any intention of giving up his U.S. citizenship, he maintained that he would not reverse his choice. That day, MBC cameras also captured fans lingering around the airport. The reports stated there were about 40 of them. It wasn’t that his stage had disappeared because there was no one waiting for him. Even though fans were at the airport, the singer himself could not enter the country. About six hours later, he headed back to the United States. The press conference he intended to hold in Seoul was canceled. His domestic schedule, planned just the day before, ended inside the airport. The concerns he expressed himself at the airport were more specific. He said that after finishing his public service, he would be nearly thirty years old. He worried that his time as a dance singer would pass while his activities were halted. He worried about the time away from the stage, but the gap in his domestic career became far longer than the service period he mentioned. This part fueled the shock at the time. People serving in the military also have to put their work and personal lives on hold. Others also faced situations where they had to leave their jobs or delay their studies. Those who could not be convinced even after hearing Steve Yoo's explanation drew the line right there. Even then, there were counterarguments that the entry ban was excessive. They asked why such measures were taken only against Steve Yoo compared to others who chose U.S. citizenship. In subsequent lawsuits, how long he can be barred from entering remains a key issue. There was one instance where his entry was exceptionally permitted. The following year, in 2003, he entered temporarily to attend his father-in-law's funeral. The Ministry of Justice permitted a stay of three days. In reality, he entered on June 26 and left the following day. According to an SBS report at the time, he filled out an application to lift the entry ban at an airport office. At the same airport where he couldn't pass the previous year, he this time received confirmation of his special permitted purpose. He was only allowed to pay his respects, with conditions that he could not appear on broadcasts or engage in entertainment activities. As soon as the funeral was over, he returned to the United States. However, this did not mean his life and work abroad collapsed entirely. Steve Yoo found a different stage. He continued his musical activities in China and expanded his scope to include acting. This is where Jackie Chan appears. Steve Yoo signed a contract with the company led by Jackie Chan and began appearing in movies. He said that even this journey was not smooth from the start. In a past interview, Steve Yoo said that Jackie Chan had called him first. It was an invitation to have a meal together, but he claimed there was no further contact for several months after that. Just when I thought the call was from the fourth floor, they reached out again, and we agreed to meet at a hotel. But this time , I went to the wrong hotel. There were two hotels with the same name, so I ended up over two hours late for the appointment. I looked back and realized I almost missed an opportunity I’d waited years for right from our first meeting. At that meeting, I told them I wanted to become an actor. I said that after joining Jackie Chan’s project, I started over as a rookie following the director’s orders. I also confessed that early in my career in China, I was picky about stages and vehicles, and even asked them to bring dancers from Korea. But for that interview, they say I carried my own luggage and came alone by taxi. Broadcasting was not the only channel through which he tried to reach Korean fans again. There was also music . In 2007, he released a Korean-language album in Korea. It had been six years since his previous full-length album. His agency at the time explained that Korean fans had traveled all the way to China to buy the albums and requested that he record them in Korean. The agency introduced it as a tribute album for his domestic fans. That album even included a recording from the time he was denied entry at the airport. The broadcast review also yielded unexpected results. According to reports on the agency’s announcement at the time, some broadcasters passed the review for this album. What one broadcaster pointed out was the car brand mentioned in the song . They were judging whether the music met broadcast standards, separate from the singer’s entry issues. However, the program covering his life in the United States could not be aired. Previously, a cable music channel had prepared a documentary but canceled the broadcast amid backlash. In his prime, just his appearance alone created anticipation for a program. Now, the production team that decided on his appearance had to explain themselves afterward. There was also an incident where his new start in China almost appeared on terrestrial television. It was when MBC was preparing a program about Jackie Chan. The story of Yoo Seung-jun, who had started working with Jackie Chan, was also filmed. It wasn’t a one-hour special about Yoo Seung-jun. According to reports at the time, his appearance was only about two minutes long. Before the broadcast, objections were posted on the program’s bulletin board. The production team eventually decided to remove that part. The main producer explained that although it was a scene captured naturally while filming Jackie Chan, they decided not to air it, considering the response. Overseas, he was an actor discussing his next project with new agencies, but in Korea, even a short appearance became subject to editing. Conversely, the movie "Little Big Soldier" was released in Korean theaters. Audiences could see Yoo Seung-jun appearing alongside Jackie Chan on the screen. His career as an actor in China was delivered to domestic audiences in the form of a film. He also attended the MAMA awards held in Hong Kong alongside Jackie Chan. It was a music awards ceremony created in Korea, but the venue was Hong Kong. At the time , he was there to promote his film " Chinese Zodiac" and stated he had no plans to resume activities in Korea. Even for the same Korean entertainment event, his attendance became possible once the venue changed. His path, which had been blocked at domestic airports, continued through overseas venues like that. Then, a scene appears where he kneels directly in front of Korean viewers. It was an internet live broadcast held in Hong Kong in May 2015 . As the broadcast began, he bowed his head and apologized. He also expressed that if he could turn back time, he would join the military. It had been 13 years since he was barred from entering the country. Answering the host's questions for over an hour, he explained his choices and what he had been through . He explained that he hadn't planned everything out from the very beginning before leaving. He claimed his family lived in the US, and that family influence and the situation at the time played a role in the citizenship issue. However, SBS aired an interview with him that was filmed back in 2003. At that time, Yoo Seung-jun said he didn't think giving up his citizenship to join the military was the way to heal the wounds . What he once called "not a solution" was now being talked about as a choice he wished he could reverse. A new story emerged during the apology broadcast. He claimed that the previous year, he had looked into ways to renounce his US citizenship and join the Korean military . But he said he was told it wasn't possible due to his age. The Military Manpower Administration's response was different. They stated that Yoo Seung-jun had never officially inquired about enlisting. They added that as a foreigner, he wasn't even subject to mandatory military service. The broadcast, which was meant to discuss the sincerity of his apology, turned into a debate over which agency he had contacted. A week later, Yoo Seung-jun held a second live broadcast. This time , he explained the contact route in more detail. He claimed he didn't inquire officially with the Military Manpower Administration, but spoke with an Army Major General through an acquaintance. He said he expressed his wish to enlist and was later told by the acquaintance that it was difficult due to his age. He also corrected the information about his age that he mentioned in the first broadcast. Reactions ranged from welcoming the long-awaited apology to criticizing it for coming after he was already past enlistment age. Another unpleasant incident occurred as well. After the second live broadcast ended, the screen went black, but the microphone wasn't turned off. A conversation containing profanity was broadcast as it was. Reports on the day couldn't confirm whose voice it was, and the production company clarified it was the staff talking. They explained that Yoo Seung-jun was not the one using the profanity. In the end, a broadcast meant to convey an apology turned into a situation where the production team had to explain an audio mishap. His explanation of his departure during the apology broadcast was also refuted by the Military Manpower Administration. Yoo Seung-jun claimed he only learned later that two employees from the Military Manpower Administration had stood as his guarantors and that he did not know who they were. The Military Manpower Administration clarified that the actual guarantors were not their employees, but two of his personal acquaintances. They also added that he had received permission only after pledging to return following his performance in Japan. The broadcast, meant to explain his efforts to return to Korea, ended up reigniting controversy over the promises he made when he first left. Afterward, Yoo Seung-jun applied for a visa at the Consulate General in Los Angeles. It was an F-4 visa, which allows overseas compatriots with foreign citizenship to stay and work in Korea. It is commonly referred to as the F-4 visa. He was seeking a judgment on whether to be issued the visa after expressing his desire to return during the apology broadcast. This is where counterarguments emerged. People asked why he applied for a visa with such broad scope for stay and employment when a short-term visit would have sufficed. This also led to suspicions that he was trying to return to make money. Yoo Seung-jun clarified that he had no plans for profitable activities in Korea, and his representatives explained that they chose to challenge the legal status of an overseas compatriot at the advice of his lawyers. In response to claims that he could have just come as a tourist, Yoo Seung-jun's side retorted as follows . They argued that if there is an entry ban, one could be rejected at the airport even if arriving visa-free. The Consulate General refused to issue the visa. Yoo Seung-jun filed a lawsuit to have that decision overturned. He did not win from the very beginning. He lost the first trial and the subsequent one. The potential impact on those fulfilling their military service and concerns over a trend of draft evasion appeared in the court's judgment. In an interview with the JoongAng Ilbo, he described the moment he heard the news of his first loss. He said it was right after he had suffered a dangerous accident while filming an action movie in China. He explained that while practicing a scene jumping from a cargo ship to a boat, a wire snapped and he fell into the sea. He said he was drying off when he received a call that he had lost the trial in Korea. He considered dropping the lawsuit, but said he appealed because his lawyer insisted it was a case worth fighting to the end. The way he conveyed his position to the court was also different. Since it was difficult to appear in a Korean court to explain in person, he said he filmed and submitted a video containing the circumstances behind his enlistment comments and the reasons for acquiring citizenship. The length was reportedly about 15 minutes. He created materials to show the court separate from the explanation released on the broadcast. The singer who used to speak to the camera was now explaining his choices to a judge. The trial that continued in that manner changed direction at the Supreme Court. The issue became whether the Consulate General that reviewed his visa had made its own independent judgment. The basis for the refusal was the entry ban decision made a long time ago. The Supreme Court took issue with the fact that the application was mechanically denied simply because of that prior decision. The point was that the Consulate General should have personally weighed the current circumstances and the disadvantages to the individual. They also deemed the procedure of notifying the denial by phone rather than in writing to be problematic. Following this judgment, a retrial was ordered. While the retrial was ongoing, a new song that had been prepared was canceled just before its release. Yoo Seung-jun announced on the Chinese social media platform Weibo that he would release his new song "Another Day" the following day. Even a teaser was released, but as public criticism arose, the scheduled music distributor took down the teaser and canceled the release. This was in November 2018. But that wasn't the end of it. About two months later, the new album was actually released. It was made available on domestic music sites on January 18, 2019 . It contained four tracks and H-Eugene participated in the production. Anyone who wanted to listen to Yoo Seung-jun's songs could now play them on these sites . In the summer of that same year, his prime-era performances were replayed in an unexpected place. SBS began continuously streaming old K-pop broadcasts on YouTube. Stages from the late 90s and early 2000s were featured one after another. Singers from that era appeared in succession, and Yoo Seung-jun's performances were among them . Viewers chatted with each other while watching the old songs together. However , in a chat log introduced by Maeil Business Newspaper at the time, there was a reaction calling Yoo Seung-jun's performance a "concert by a foreign artist." Watching his old stage as a Korean singer reminded them of his current status as an American. The songs and dances were the same as before, but the way some viewers perceived the performance had changed. In this situation, the production team of SBS's "Night of Real Entertainment" traveled to the U.S. to meet him. Yoo Seung-jun re-explained his old remarks about enlisting in the military. He stated that it wasn't as if he had voluntarily stepped forward to join the army from the very beginning. His explanation was as follows. A reporter he knew asked if he should join the army, and he replied that if he were to go, he would go. He claimed that the talk about the Marine Corps also came up during that conversation. He argued that the matter escalated after it was reported as a voluntary enlistment story. His point was that he didn't deceive anyone by claiming he would join the army after already having obtained citizenship. At the same time, he said that his intention to join the military had been sincere. He also admitted that he later changed his mind and disappointed people by failing to keep his promise. However , the explanation that a reporter asked the question first does not change the fact that he later changed his mind. Articles conveying his explanation that the media blew it out of proportion also included criticism that he was shifting the blame. The interview drew significant attention. According to reports at the time, viewership ratings for a portion of the show "Hanbam" rose by 1 percentage point from the previous week to reach 7%, according to the ratings agency TNMS. At the moment the Yoo Seung-jun interview aired, it climbed to 8%, becoming the show's peak minute of that day. The power of his name remained enough to move viewership ratings just by appearing on screen after such a long time. However, the broadcast clip was also met with criticism, questioning whether it was necessary to go all the way to the U.S. to hear his explanation. The production team explained that they wanted to hear the story directly from the person involved. Victory in his first lawsuit was finalized in March 2020. For Yoo Seung-jun, this meant returning to the Consulate General with the court's decision in hand. However, the answer that came back a few months later was another rejection. The government's position was that they had made a new judgment after synthesizing relevant laws, regulations, and various circumstances. Yoo Seung-jun's side filed another lawsuit, claiming the spirit of the verdict had not been followed. At the same parliamentary audit, comments were even made by the Commissioner of the Military Manpower Administration. Then-Commissioner Mo Jong-hwa stated at the National Assembly that the entry ban should be maintained . He also stated that he viewed him as an American, not a Korean. Yoo Seung-jun countered by saying that while he accepts criticism for his choices, such a long-term entry restriction is discriminatory and a human rights violation. The stage of the conflict expanded from broadcast interviews to National Assembly responses and public rebuttals. By the end of the year, an even stronger rebuttal video was released. A bill was proposed in the National Assembly to strengthen entry restrictions for people who changed their nationality to avoid military service. It was dubbed the so-called " Yoo Seung-jun Prevention Act." Yoo Seung-jun reacted to this legislative movement on YouTube. In a video lasting about 40 minutes, he criticized politicians and the media while bringing up several current issues. Among them were these words. Why should I apologize to the nation? I made a promise to my fans. In the past, he knelt and apologized, but this time he was arguing about who he should be apologizing to. What he said was that it was a promise to his fans. However, the government took issue with the impact on the military service system and other individuals obligated to serve. The scope of the responsibilities they were talking about was different from the start. The turning point that emerged in the second lawsuit was age. The old law applicable to Yoo Seung-jun included a provision granting residency status after a certain age, even for those who lost their nationality to avoid military service. The standard considered by the court was 38 years of age. The appellate court ruled that unless there were special circumstances, such as concerns about harming national interests, residency status could not be continuously denied solely based on past military service issues after that age. In this lawsuit as well, the first trial was unfavorable to Yoo Seung-jun. However, it was overturned on appeal, and his victory was finalized by the Supreme Court in November 2023. Yoo Seung-jun's side has applied for a visa again based on this verdict. However, the third notice of refusal included a new reason. The consulate stated that after reviewing the matter with the Ministry of Justice, they maintained the entry ban because his actions after the 2020 refusal could potentially harm national interests. This was in 2024. The grounds for refusal expanded from his 2002 nationality choice to his subsequent words and actions. It was not revealed that any single video was the specific cause. Yoo Seung-jun's lawyer countered that his recent words, actions , and the social reaction had already been examined in the second trial. They argued that bringing up those circumstances again to deny a visa is a measure that ignores the final court ruling. They also asked if an individual can be continuously barred just because many people dislike him. Even after winning two previous final court rulings , a third trial began. The court first examined whether the new reasons for refusal put forward by the government were valid. Yoo Seung-jun contested not only the visa refusal but also the entry ban decision itself. The court stated that the entry ban is an internal decision of an administrative agency and did not delve into the merits of the ban . However, it ruled that the actual measure of not issuing the visa should be revoked. The first trial ruling last August did not accept the reasons for refusal put forward by the government. It determined that the disadvantage Yoo Seung-jun suffers is too great compared to the public interest the government seeks to protect. The court also pointed out the issue of restricting him for an exceptionally long time compared to military service evaders. It was a judgment on how long a restriction based on past choices can be maintained. The court clarified that this judgment does not mean it approves of Yoo Seung-jun's past behavior. It meant they would distinguish between evaluating his past choices and judging whether the current visa refusal complies with the law. There was also a change in Yoo Seung-jun's own words this June. He uploaded a video to his channel titled, "I'm going to stop now." He called Korea his birthplace and his spiritual home, but said that entering the country now carries little meaning. He explained that although he apologized and tried to clarify, his sincerity did not seem to be properly conveyed. He said he would stop, but the appellate trial continued . Even after he expressed his feelings that the meaning of entering Korea had diminished, the judgment on whether the decision to deny the visa was correct had not ended. And on October 2nd, the appellate court also maintained the ruling to revoke the visa refusal. The answer to the question asked at the beginning lies here. Although the previous refusal was revoked due to the victory in court, the government denied him again after re-examination, leading to a lawsuit over that decision. Re-examination does not mean it is unconditionally permitted; any new refusal must also align with the purpose of the final ruling and the law. A comeback as a singer is a matter that remains for later. Even if the visa is issued, the broadcaster must decide on an appearance, hold a performance, and the audience must buy tickets. A victory in court does not automatically bring back the performance opportunities of his prime. However, the story did not end with the appellate court this time either. On October 26, the Consul General in Los Angeles filed an appeal. This means the third visa lawsuit is now headed to the Supreme Court. Therefore, this case is not currently in a state where a final victory has been confirmed . Nor has any date been set for his entry into Korea. It is impossible to know when Yoo Seung-jun's next domestic stage will be. The schedule attached to his name right now is not a comeback date, but an ongoing trial.
