Sweden, a welfare state envied by the entire world. The government recently announced with pride that shooting crimes have decreased. But wait, was Sweden a country with so many shooting crimes? Hello. This is Kim Yoon. What comes to your mind when you think of Sweden? A country with excellent social welfare. The home of the Nobel Prize, and IKEA. But there is a story that Sweden has been suffering from gun violence for the last 10 years. As some of you might know, it’s quite a different image from the Sweden we usually think of. For example, in 2023, Sweden held the disgraceful title of having the highest rate of gun deaths in the EU. In 2022, there were reportedly 391 gun-related incidents throughout the year. That means there was at least one shooting incident every single day. Swedish police even stated at the time that the country was experiencing a level of violence unprecedented in its history. But it’s not just the numbers that have grown; the nature of the crimes is also quite strange. For instance, there were 162 bombing incidents in 2018. By 2025, this increased to over 600 cases. When we think of violent crimes, we usually imagine the use of knives or guns in countries where they are legal, but bombings make you wonder if this is more like terrorism. It’s a method primarily used by gangs and criminal organizations. They often leave bombs or throw grenades at apartment entrances, restaurants, or the homes and residential areas of their targets. Gang crime has increased significantly, and the Swedish police estimate there are about 17,000 people currently in these gangs. And if you count everyone involved for various reasons, nearly 60,000 people are linked to gang-related crime. Now, when shooting crimes become this frequent, everyone looks for the root cause. And one of the causes often cited is the issue of immigration. According to statistics published by the Swedish National Council for Crime Prevention, there are figures that seem to support this. This report classifies people by their background and provides statistics on the ratio of crime suspects. Those born in Sweden with both parents also born in Sweden—native Swedes. Those born in Sweden, but with one parent born abroad. And those born in Sweden, but with both parents born abroad— second-generation immigrants. And finally, those who were born abroad and immigrated themselves, the first generation. They categorized them this way to see what the ratio of crime suspects was. The results showed that among these crime suspects, native-born Swedes accounted for 51. 4%, or thereabouts. For those born outside of Sweden, the first-generation immigrants , they accounted for 32.9%. Looking at just these results, one might think, " Oh, the crime rate for first-generation immigrants seems a bit high, but native-born Swedes still make up the largest share." However, you shouldn't just take these numbers at face value. You have to compare them to the share these people represent in the total Swedish population. In reality, native-born Swedes—those born in Sweden with both parents also born in Sweden—account for 72%of the total population. So, the fact that they account for just over 50%of the crime suspects actually means their relative rate is low. And for first-generation immigrants, those born abroad who came to Sweden, they account for 18.4%of the total Swedish population. Since they make up over 30%of total crime suspects , one could say their crime rate is high. This trend was even more pronounced in organized crime, or gang-related activities. A Swedish newspaper conducted an in-depth investigation of all 432 individuals who were arrested, charged, or convicted in connection with these gang crimes. Out of these 432 people, 85% were found to have a foreign background . It is said that 41%were born abroad and immigrated to Sweden. Looking at this, it’s understandable why native Swedes might feel that immigration is the problem. So, why is Sweden suddenly struggling so much with the issue of immigration? Like other European countries, Sweden is a nation that accepts immigrants. But just like other European nations, they faced a major turning point in their immigration policy. That was in 2015. Because of the refugees crossing into Europe that year, various problems began to emerge within European society. And how many people came to Sweden in 2015 to apply for asylum? It was a staggering 160,000 people. You might not fully grasp the scale of 163,000 people just by the number. At the time, the population of Sweden was 9.8 million. This means that 1.7%of the total population applied for asylum. While the scale is a major issue in itself, the bigger problem is that so many people arrived so suddenly in such a short period of time. In other words, regardless of whether Sweden was socially and emotionally prepared to accept these refugees, its infrastructure and economy simply weren't robust enough to handle the sudden influx of refugees and immigrants. But what should we take note of here? The statistics I mentioned earlier are from 2015 to 2018 . In fact, most of the people included in those figures had already come to Sweden before 2010. This means that the figures for when the truly massive wave of refugees poured into Swedish society haven't even been calculated yet. Another terrifying aspect regarding gang crimes, shootings, or bombings in Sweden is that children are being hired as contract killers. So, how old are these children? It is said that some are as young as ten. They are given these murder commissions, and the money paid to these children is said to be up to about 150,000 kronor, which is around 20 million Korean won. Then why children? It is because it's difficult to punish them since they are children. I hear that a revised law has been passed now. But at the time, children under the age of 15 did not receive criminal punishment. So, you could say it's quite similar to the issue of juvenile offenders that is currently a point of controversy in our country as well. From the perspective of gangs or those requesting contract killings, they preferred children because they could pay them less, and even if they were caught, the criminal liability would be relatively light. Since they were recruiting children for contract killings, the recruitment methods, as you might guess, often took place through social media or even games like Roblox or Fortnite. And the frequent explosions I mentioned earlier are also partly related to this. Why? Because they are children, it’s not easy for them to use firearms to shoot and kill people. However, placing explosives or throwing hand grenades is relatively easy even for children to do. As these incidents keep occurring, it naturally has an impact on Swedish politics as well. Seeing these statistics and the criminal cases in the news, the Swedish people naturally demand stronger immigration policies, refugee policies, and criminal justice policies. And these demands eventually led to a very surprising election result in 2022. Like in other European countries, parties started to emerge in Sweden arguing that immigration should be strictly controlled or that the doors should be closed altogether. That is the Sweden Democrats. And the Sweden Democrats made great strides in the 2022 general election. In that election , the Social Democratic Party, a giant of the left, won about 30%of the vote. They took first place. The party that took second place was the Sweden Democrats. With about 20%of the vote, they secured 73 seats in total. In short, you could say the SD came in first within the right-wing bloc. However, Sweden is a country that uses a proportional representation system. In countries with proportional representation, it's not easy for one party to win a majority. So, after election results are in, parties with similar leanings often form coalitions to establish a cabinet. The Sweden Democrats were founded in 1988. At the time, some members had neo-Nazi ties and held very radical views, so they were labeled a far-right party. Europe actually has many parties like this, and mainstream parties typically declare after elections that they won't join hands with such far-right or far-left parties. They build a firewall , promising not to form a coalition government with them. The Sweden Democrats, who had been ostracized, began a full-scale rebranding in 2005 to improve their image. Instead of other issues, they focused solely on immigration policy, refugee policy, and the war on crime. Coincidentally, the Swedish public was just starting to realize the severity of these issues, so their message began to gain traction . They grew gradually after that, and the 2022 election was where they truly achieved a major breakthrough. Although they placed first in the right-wing bloc, the Sweden Democrats were unable to join the cabinet. Other parties in the right-wing bloc strongly opposed the Sweden Democrats entering the cabinet. In other words, it was like saying, "If they come in, we're out." It was basically like that. So, the Sweden Democrats didn't join the cabinet but were promised that the government would adopt policies aligning with their key agendas, such as immigration, refugees, and tougher criminal penalties. Since they are the majority party, shouldn't they be the ones trying to form the cabinet? To explain what this means, there are three traditional conservative parties. The largest one was the Moderate Party, and the combined number of seats they had with the Moderates was higher. One would assume they would divide cabinet positions based on the proportion of seats held. That is often the case in many instances. That’s true, but fundamentally, that is the norm. In a parliamentary system, the most important thing for the ruling party bloc is to secure a majority of seats within their coalition. But here, if parties like the Liberal Party refuse to join because of them, they can't reach a majority, so they had to make compromises after calculating the numbers. They basically said, "We'll do whatever you want." They couldn't quite join the cabinet, which left the Sweden Democrats with a dilemma. In the end, while they didn't join the cabinet, they pushed hard for strict immigration , refugee, and criminal justice policies, prioritizing Swedish values and cultural assimilation over multiculturalism. So, in a sense, the existing parties adopted the very things the Sweden Democrats were advocating for. In that respect, their agenda became mainstream policy. It could even be seen as quite a significant achievement. Was that the reason? Anyway, as immigration and criminal justice policies have toughened, Sweden's current crime rate has dropped. According to an analysis, the number of crimes prevented and intercepted by Swedish police has also increased significantly. By utilizing wiretapping devices, drones, and other tools, the rate of stopping crimes before they happen has gone way up. Now , with Sweden's public safety improving , it naturally seems to have influenced the political landscape. In fact, there was a general election in Sweden recently. So you'd think the Sweden Democrats would have won again, but news has come in that they lost a whopping 11 seats compared to the last election. If they had such success, why did the Sweden Democrats lose so many seats? We'll need more time and a more detailed analysis to be sure, though. For now, the talk is that the established parties essentially took the agenda that the Sweden Democrats held. The Sweden Democrats were a trendy, edgy party, but as big, established parties adopted their platform and normalized it, the unique appeal they once had vanished. And ironically, the fact that crime rates went down and were somewhat resolved actually had a negative impact on the Sweden Democrats. In the past, people would agree and support the Sweden Democrats when they raised these issues , but now that the problems are being solved, the party isn't as attractive as it used to be.
