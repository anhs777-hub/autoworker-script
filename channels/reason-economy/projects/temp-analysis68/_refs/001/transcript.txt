I’m sure you’ve told your children at least once to study hard and get a decent job at a good company. We all assumed that manual labor jobs would be the first to disappear. But right now, in Korea, the exact opposite is happening. Over the past four years, 270,000 jobs for young people have vanished. Yet, in the same sectors, jobs for people in their 50s have actually increased by 170,000. The doors are opening for the parents ' generation, but closing for their children. There are even professions now where people pass the country's hardest exams only to find nowhere to start their first day of work. Today, we are counting down the seven jobs in Korea that are closing their doors the fastest. Number one is the very profession everyone was rushing into just a few years ago, thinking it guaranteed a lifetime of stability. And there is one identical pattern hidden within these seven jobs. Once you realize it, job market news will start looking completely different to you. If you watch until the end, I’ll show you how to tell in 30 seconds if the path your child is choosing is opening up or closing down. Let’s start right away with number seven. Number seven is the taxi driver. Earlier this year, a taxi carrying passengers late at night in Gangnam turns through an intersection. But it’s not a human turning the steering wheel. It’s a self-driving taxi. They started charging fares in April, and by August, the fleet had grown to 19. By November, taxis with completely empty driver seats are expected to be on the road in Sangam. When you see news like this, you all think the same thing, don’t you? That taxi drivers will eventually be pushed out by autonomous vehicles. But that’s not the case. 19 cars won't push anyone out. It’s still just a pilot operation running in a few streets of Gangnam late at night. The real reason taxi drivers are decreasing is something else entirely. There are no young people coming in to take the wheel. Ever noticed that taxi drivers seem a bit older lately when you catch a ride? That’s not just in your head. There are 164,000 private taxi drivers nationwide. Out of those, how many do you think are under the age of 30? Just seventy. It might be hard to grasp the scale of that. Suppose you try to hail a private taxi every single morning on your way to work. Statistically, you’d have to wait over six years to run into a driver under thirty just once. Even if you count everyone in their 30s, it’s less than one in a hundred. The other end of the spectrum is overflowing. More than one out of every two private taxi drivers is over the age of sixty-five. Company-owned taxi fleets are in an even more urgent situation. The number of drivers has plummeted over the last decade. The reason is simple. The take-home pay for the work put in just isn't enough. During the three years of the pandemic, three out of ten drivers left the profession. We thought they would return once the social distancing rules were lifted. But the drivers who left hardly ever returned. Then, who is sitting in those empty driver's seats now? A taxi driver in Seoul says that foreign drivers, whom we didn't see before, have filled the spots where his colleagues quit one after another this year. The companies are even saying that two out of every ten applicants are foreigners. Even before self-driving cars arrived, taxis were already losing their future drivers. At least with taxis, it was a case of people not coming on their own. The sixth case is different. It's a place where calls are diverted to machines even while people are still sitting there wearing headsets. The sixth case is call center agents. You call a credit card company. How may I help you ? It’s not a human voice. It answers fixed questions well, but if the situation gets even slightly complicated, it doesn't understand and sends you back to the same menu. After going around twice or three times, exhausted, you finally press to connect to an agent, and then you have to wait a long time just to hear a human voice. It’s that run-around everyone has experienced at least once. So, how many of those many agents are left now? Looking just at telecom call centers, more than half have disappeared. The number of agents, which was over 31,000 in 2019, was barely 14,000 last year. Out of two people who were sitting side by side wearing headsets six years ago, only one is left now. People have been leaving credit card and home shopping call centers every year as well. What's answering the phone in those empty seats is AI. One major bank has AI answering four out of every ten incoming consultation calls. There are also banks where AI makes 98%of the calls to customers. Some say they will double the number of consultations handled by AI by November this year. There is a hidden structure here. Nearly 90%of bank call center agents are not actually bank employees. They belong to subcontracting companies. So, for the bank, it ends by just reducing the service contract. They can quietly reduce the number of people without directly firing anyone. Then, have things become easier for those who remain? It was the exact opposite. One bank agent confessed this. AI takes all the easy calls, leaving only the difficult ones for the people. Before, 70 calls a day was reasonable, but now it's 100 to 120. If you work for 8 hours, it's an amount you have to answer every 4 minutes without a break. A labor union survey also showed that after AI was introduced, the complaints each person receives actually increased , and six out of ten said their working conditions worsened. The phone keeps ringing the same, but the number of people answering has dropped to less than half. You might think that consultation is work with fixed answers , so it's doable for machines. But the fifth case is work with no fixed answers. It was a job that required fluency in two languages plus extra specialized training. Number 5 is translators and interpreters. This past July, an app that sells e-books of classic literature bowed its head in apology. They admitted to selling 730 books translated by AI while keeping that fact hidden. When new books appeared too quickly and too cheaply, users started raising questions, and the app eventually had to issue refunds . But for me, this part was even scarier than the fact that people were angry. It meant that they had piled up 730 books in less than a year of being open. It means that machines are already hitting speeds that a human translator could never achieve. Many of you have probably spared no expense when it comes to your children's English tutoring fees. From English-speaking kindergartens to language study abroad, everyone believed that mastering a foreign language would guarantee a living for life. But looking at this year's statistics, the number of people in their 20s working in jobs categorized as translators and writers has dropped by 20.6%in a year. Where five people worked a year ago, now there are four. That one missing person could be your own child. Job openings looking for people to do this work have also decreased by nearly a third. The calculations of those creating AI are the same. Microsoft analyzed 200,000 cases of how people used AI. The number one profession that overlaps the most with what AI does. It was interpreters and translators. The educational side has already shifted its direction as well. Several graduate schools for translation and interpretation have newly opened courses on AI and translation. Then, what kind of work are translators losing first? The story of a freelancer who does legal and corporate interpretation perfectly paints the picture. They said legal work remains the same, but work coming in from companies has been cut in half. Jobs where a human absolutely must stand are holding up, but other tasks are disappearing first. The remaining work has also changed in nature. Instead of translating from scratch, there is more work refining sentences that AI has already translated. This past spring, there was even a report on the main evening news that AI does the translating and humans only do the editing. The job of translation remains , but the work that used to look for a translator is disappearing first. You might say that up to this point, these are things a machine could handle anyway. But number 4 is a profession where the government directly administers an exam to issue a license. Perhaps there might even be one of those certificates sitting in your drawer right now. Number 4 is licensed real estate agents. In 2021, nearly 400,000 people applied for this exam. That’s how it earned the nickname, the "national certificate." Office workers and stay-at-home moms who finished raising their kids all opened their workbooks, calling it a life-changing license. There are likely quite a few of you who were sitting in the exam hall that autumn. But try taking a walk on the first floor of your local shopping plaza this year. You might start seeing a few dark signs among the rows of real estate offices. In Seoul alone, one in six brokerage offices has disappeared over the past three years. Even the number of applicants for the license has dropped. Last year's applicants were 165,000, a 59%decrease in four years. It's easy to visualize this way. If ten classrooms were full at the test site in 2021, last year only four were filled, and there was no need to open the other six . The number of real estate agents who opened new offices last year was also 9,150. That’s the lowest since 1998, when the aftermath of the financial crisis was at its peak. For three years , more offices have closed than opened, and the number of active real estate agents is at its lowest in six and a half years. There are 550,000 license holders, but only one in five is actually working with a sign up. If the license is so common, why have fewer people hung up their signs? There is only one answer. Houses aren't selling. Apartment transactions have dropped by more than a third compared to a few years ago when they were selling well. More people are buying, selling, or renting homes directly through neighborhood apps like Karrot without using an agent. One brokerage in Mapo, Seoul, says they used to have about ten sales a month, but now they barely get one or two. They are struggling just to pay the office rent. It's said that many agents hang on for a while, close temporarily, and eventually take down their signs without even getting their key money back. This made me think about something. This license wasn't originally meant to be used right away. It was a kind of insurance to keep in a drawer just in case. But even when the day comes to take out that insurance, the clients to accept it are decreasing . The license in the drawer remains the same, but fewer people are taking it out to hang up a sign every year. At least real estate agents can blame the economy. The third place is different. It's a place where the chairs in front of the counters became empty in the year they earned the most money ever. Third place is bank employees. Many of you must have felt relieved when you heard your child got a job at a bank, a stable position in the city. But have you seen this notice on the glass doors of your neighborhood bank lately? "As of this date, we will be merging with a nearby branch." It means the counter where you pulled a number and sat on the sofa waiting is closing. In less than five years, 904 bank branches have shut their doors. It means that in some neighborhood, the ticket machine turned off more than three times a week. The pace has actually accelerated. Last year, the branches closed from January to September alone exceeded the total from the previous year. How bad must it be for the government to have tightened regulations this March to prevent banks from closing branches at will? So, was it because the banks weren't doing well ? It is the exact opposite. Last year, the four major banks earned the most money in their history. It’s just that fewer people are coming to the branch counters. When was the last time you went to a bank counter? It has probably been quite a while. At one major bank, 90%of customer banking is already done through their mobile app. The hand that used to pull a number ticket has been replaced by a finger on an app. A bank official put it exactly like this. We need more digital talent, but fewer counter staff. However, in the very year they earned the most money, they hired even fewer new employees. New hiring at the four major banks was 1,880 in 2023. Last year, it was 1,170. In just two years, where they used to hire ten people, they only hired six. The number of new open recruits in the first half of this year fell to 485. Conversely, the exit door has been wide open. Last year, the number of people who left the five major banks through voluntary retirement was the highest in recent years. Some banks even lowered the voluntary retirement age to those born in 1986, who are in their late 30s. The average voluntary severance package is nearly 350 million won. When combined with their original severance pay, they leave with an average of over 500 million won. One bank even added either tuition support for their children or re-employment subsidies on top of that. They have been sending out employees as young as 40 with a large sum of money while reducing the number of new recruits every year. Even so, the bank door was something the company opened and closed on its own. Rank number 2 is a profession you can only enter by passing one of the country's most difficult exams. But the real problem was what happened after passing. Rank number 2 is an accountant. Last year, there were 1,200 final passers of the CPA exam. It is a certificate they received after spending several years in the library. However, just receiving the certificate doesn't make you an accountant right away. You have to learn practical work at a place like an accounting firm for over a year to finally be able to stamp documents in your own name. So, the hurdle after passing is...finding a place to learn. How many people had cleared this hurdle by late October, about a month and a half after the results were announced? 338 people. If four study peers had studied together for years and passed together, it meant only one of them had found a place to work by the time autumn deepened. By this spring, after the year had passed, the number of people who couldn't find a place was more than double what it was at this time last year. Why were there so few spots available? It’s because the accounting firms that were supposed to take them in slashed their hiring of new recruits. One of the largest accounting firms lowered the number of new hires per department from over 20 to single digits. Eventually, the government stepped in this May. They decided to expand the places where passers can learn the work to include the National Assembly, the courts, and the National Pension Service. If they can't find a place to work for over two years, the accounting association will now pair them up with accounting firms directly. In those vacant spots, major accounting firms are now deploying AI. Reviewing transaction details, finding odd numbers, reading contracts, and drafting reports—these were all tasks entry-level accountants used to learn by flipping through stacks of paper. I'm sure some of you are thinking this right now. Isn't accounting a profession protected by law anyway? That is correct. By law, only accountants can perform corporate audits. AI cannot stamp the audit report on their behalf. But that very fact has determined exactly where AI will step in. The law only protects the person who finally affixes the seal. The positions where people used to learn the ropes while flipping through documents were never protected by law. One accountant mentioned that with fewer new hires, the workload shifted to mid-level staff, making them even busier. There simply weren't enough desks for the newcomers to learn at. As there were fewer places to go, the government has been cutting the number of new recruits for two years. Even so, 1,150 people who passed the exam received their certificates earlier this month. It's heartbreaking enough that they have nowhere to go after passing, but the number one profession is even worse. Just five years ago, it was a job where companies competed to recruit you by raising salaries. In February 2021, the gaming company Nexon raised the starting salary for new developers to 50 million won. Before even a month passed, Krafton countered with 60 million won. It was a war to recruit developers. Liberal arts students and office workers alike flocked to coding academies for short-term courses. The number one job that people said would guarantee you’d never go hungry again is that of a new developer. Five years later, this year, we compared the number of employees at fifty-three domestic software companies with last year. The total increase was just 14 people. It amounts to barely one new person for every four companies combined. This year, for the first time, there were more companies that reduced staff than those that increased it. The number of developers in their 20s also dropped by about one in every ten over the past year. Even when looking at new graduate job postings on a recruitment site by industry, IT and telecommunications saw the sharpest decline last year. Two out of every three jobs disappeared. There is also a symbolic scene. Last October, one coding education company erased the word "coding" from its name entirely. Sparta Coding Club became just Sparta Club. They say their students have also shifted from job seekers to working professionals. A consultant who participated in corporate downsizing projects put it bluntly. It costs less to use AI than to train a new hire. That was the part that hurt the most. Young people who were learning how to give tasks to machines are now losing their seats to those very machines. Now , let's line up these seven professions . Taxi drivers, call center agents, translators, real estate agents, bank tellers, accountants, and developers; the reasons—young people not joining, machines answering phones, houses not selling—were all different. However, there was only one way they were disappearing. While some places, like banks, sent people off with severance pay, there was something else that happened exactly the same way in all seven areas. The positions of those who left were not filled, and no new people were brought in. The rule that ties these seven jobs together. It wasn't about layoffs; it was about not hiring. Layoffs make the news, but not hiring makes no sound at all. So why did it start specifically with the positions for young people? An employment research team leader at the Bank of Korea explains it like this. AI mainly takes over tasks that can be organized into manuals, and young people are the ones who do a lot of that work. Conversely, experienced employees handle work that requires knowing the organization’s nature and circumstances, making it hard for AI to replace them easily. To put it simply, it’s like this. There are tasks that new recruits learn and perform during their first year or two. Filing paperwork, matching vouchers, answering calls with fixed scripts, drafting translations, and writing basic code. AI has taken over exactly those tasks. So, experienced workers become more needed with AI by their side, while the entry-level positions for new recruits simply disappear. This explanation fits best with translation, accounting, and software development. The situations for taxis and banks were different, but the path for young people to enter was cut off just the same. Corporate plans point in the same direction. At the end of last year, we asked 153 HR managers which seniority level they would hire the most this year. Half chose those with four to seven years of experience. Only about eleven said they would hire new recruits. So, have office jobs themselves decreased? No. They have actually increased. As of August this year, the number of people working in office jobs was 276,000 higher than a year ago. This is the same picture as the Bank of Korea’s analysis, which shows that even in sectors AI can touch , positions for experienced seniors have grown. The offices remain the same . What disappeared was the entry-level desk—the very first seat for someone joining that office. That is why I have read this change in one line as follows . AI arrives not as a layoff notice, but as a job posting that never ends up being uploaded. Those already in their seats hear nothing, and only the young people standing at the door are feeling that cold wind first. But this does not end with these seven professions. Outside of this list, the same thing is happening on a much wider scale. The number of employed young people has been falling for 46 consecutive months as of this August. 46 months, you say? It has been decreasing without a single break since before the current college seniors even enrolled. Entry-level job postings for major companies on one recruitment site were down 42%this March compared to a year ago. It is not just because the population is shrinking. This year, the number of employed people in their 20s fell faster than the population of people in their 20s. The jobs we pointed out today are just the ones that caught our eye first in this larger trend. Of course, the Bank of Korea also said not to blame this entirely on AI. They mean that AI is simply accelerating a trend that was already crumbling as society changed. I agree with that assessment. However, regardless of what came first, the fact remains that it is the entry-level positions that are disappearing. Now, let's look at the path your child intends to take. 30 seconds is enough. First, check if the first one or two years of that job involve following a set routine. Tasks like organizing documents, data entry, answering calls with a script, or basic coding. These are exactly the kinds of tasks that AI takes over first. Second, check if the industry's job postings are filled with requirements for years of experience. It is a sign that the company is looking for experienced hires rather than teaching newcomers. Third, see if the number of people flocking to that exam or major is decreasing. It means those who went there first are turning away at the threshold. If two or more of these apply, that path is currently becoming a narrow gate. That doesn't mean your child is lacking. If diligence were the issue, the positions held by experienced seniors in the same field should have decreased as well. But those positions have actually increased . What shrank was the side trying to enter. This is why, instead of telling a child who stays up all night revising their resume to work harder, we should first help them find where the doors are still open. Fortunately, we can see where the doors are open. According to the Bank of Korea's analysis, youth employment has decreased less in sectors that use AI as a tool by their side. They were not handing work over to AI, but rather using AI to their advantage. These are fields where humans make the judgments and AI provides a helping hand. In fact, there is a shortage of personnel in the field . For construction sites alone, it is projected that there will be a shortage of nearly 300,000 skilled workers this year. Some young people are even shifting their direction toward physical technical skills. In the Seoul Technical Institute's welding course, 6 out of 10 students in the first half of this year were in their 20s or 30s. Among those taking national technical qualification exams, 4 out of 10 are in their 20s. While people said manual labor would disappear first, young people are voluntarily walking right into that very field. "Study hard and get into a decent company"—that advice wasn't wrong. However, I think it's time to add one more thing to that . In that company, will you learn work that AI will replace, or work that commands AI? If you could recommend just one path to your child, would it be office work commanding AI, even if the door is narrow? Or would it be a skilled trade where the work is physical, but there is a shortage of hands? Please leave your thoughts in the comments.
