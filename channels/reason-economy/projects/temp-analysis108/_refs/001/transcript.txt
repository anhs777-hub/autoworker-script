We have always believed that learning a skill would be enough to make a living for life. We thought simple, repetitive tasks or manual labor would be the first to disappear. However, reality has been moving in a completely different direction than we imagined. Over the past four years, jobs for people in their 50s have increased by 230,000, while over 280,000 youth jobs have vanished. Certifications earned for retirement are becoming less useful , and there are even professions where three out of four people cannot find work despite passing difficult national exams. Fortunately, not all paths are blocked. The jobs that are currently disappearing have one clear thing in common. If you know this criterion in advance, you can find jobs that are still open. So today, we will look at the seven jobs disappearing fastest in Korea right now in order. The number one spot on the list is a profession that many have scrambled to prepare for in recent years, calling it the most reliable career path regardless of their major. There is a profession where tasks that once cost 100,000 won and took two days to complete are now being done by AI. That is 7th place: designers and video editors. People used to say that if you were good at drawing and using computers, your skills would support you for life. Many of you have likely sent your children to art academies or video editing schools. Do you remember how everyone's KakaoTalk profile pictures suddenly changed to cartoon drawings last spring? If you uploaded a photo and asked for it to be drawn like a Ghibli animation, ChatGPT would do it in just a few minutes. Scroll down your KakaoTalk friend list right now. There will be one or two people who are still using those pictures as their profiles. In the past, you would have had to pay a professional illustrator to get those drawings. It is the same for YouTube videos. According to a survey from three years ago, outsourcing video editing cost 10,000 won per minute. A 10-minute video would cost 100,000 won, and editors often spent two or three days working on just one. Now, you just need to put a script into AI, and a draft edit comes out immediately. It is easy to understand if you think about photo studios before the smartphone era . Before smartphones were widespread, a roll of film had 24 frames, so we had to take each photo sparingly, drop it off for development, and go back to pick up the prints. Now, you take dozens of photos of your children or pets every single day with your phone. We take far more photos, but photo studios have closed one by one. It is the same for drawings and videos. The amount of content being created is overflowing, but the positions for people who used to get paid to make them are shrinking. Company numbers are already shifting as well. The number of design positions companies are looking to fill has dropped by nearly one in six over the past year. This figure comes from a corporate workforce survey conducted in the latter half of last year. Among the tasks designers perform , there are specific ones that are disappearing first. These are the entry-level assistant tasks, like creating multiple drafts, editing cuts, formatting subtitles, and removing backgrounds. A report from last May on projections by the Korea Employment Information Service also identified design editing assistants, along with bank tellers, as the jobs most vulnerable to AI. Even a career path once thought to last a lifetime based on skill alone is seeing its entry-level positions narrow. Now, setting aside the visual arts. What about human voices? Since it involves infusing emotion into the tone, you might think this is something AI cannot easily mimic. Just a few years ago, this was work exclusively entrusted to humans. That industry is number six: voice actors. As AI voices became more natural, the amount of work for voice actors began to shrink. Shopping mall announcements are a prime example. One voice actor shared a story in a media interview last February. Before landing a permanent position, they worked part-time doing mall announcements, but now those jobs have been replaced by AI voices. The advertising industry is reportedly similar. At first, they only used AI for sample recordings for advertisers, but now they are using it for the actual ad voice-overs. This is not all. Last autumn, KBS decided to cut its annual hiring of exclusive voice actors in half, from eight to four. The same plan included provisions for increasing the use of AI anchors and AI radio news. So, is this the end for voice actors? Not necessarily. Conversely, some voice actors have started selling their own voices. You remember MacGyver, right? The person who provided the voice for MacGyver is voice actor Bae Han-sung. Earlier this year, an AI company began working to formally license Bae Han-sung's voice to create an AI version. They are currently in the testing phase to see if it can be used for commercials or educational videos. This company allows even non-famous voice actors to buy and sell their voices. If you upload an AI version of your voice, you receive royalties whenever someone else uses it . So far, the people who have uploaded their voices this way have earned about 30 billion won. That amount has doubled in just six months. One foreign actor even claimed they earned more from this than they had from acting over the previous five years. It is not that voice work is disappearing entirely. While AI is filling the small, entry-level roles, new paths are opening for those with unique voices to sell them. If machines can even produce voices, what about translating speech into other languages? Number five is translators and interpreters. Do you know how many jobs for translators and interpreters in their 20s have disappeared in the past year? Surprisingly, the number of writers and translators in their 20s enrolled in employment insurance dropped sharply from 1,367 to 1,085. That means one in five has left the workplace. When you travel abroad these days, you just point your smartphone at a menu, and the screen instantly translates it into your language. We live in an era where machines translate languages perfectly in the blink of an eye. Have machines really taken away all the jobs from translators? There is a very interesting and painful twist hidden in these figures. Looking at the profession as a whole, the total number of people has actually increased. The real problem lies in new recruitment. One out of every three job openings for new hires has vanished without a trace. In other words, translation work hasn't disappeared, but while veteran professionals continue to work, machines have snatched up all the entry-level positions where beginners would have started. Rankings 7 through 5 were cases where machines took the jobs. This time, it is a case where the market cooled down before the machines did. There is a profession where over 550,000 people have obtained a license, yet only one in five has actually opened their own office. That is 4th place, the licensed real estate agent. You all know someone who got the license thinking they would open a real estate office in the neighborhood after retirement, right? Try asking them if they are actually using that license right now. We call a driver's license that isn't used a "closet license." Most of those real estate licenses are just gathering dust, just like a closet license. The atmosphere was completely different just a few years ago. In 2021 , when housing prices were soaring daily, 278,000 people took the exam. It was the highest number ever. But three years later, only about half that many took the test. It’s not just the number of test-takers that has dropped. Last year, the number of real estate agents who opened new offices was 9,150 . That is the lowest number since 1998, right after the Asian financial crisis. Seeing these numbers, you probably think they all went out of business because things weren't selling, right? That’s only half right. According to the Korea Association of Realtors, it’s not so much that people are closing up shop, but that fewer people are opening new ones. Then why wouldn't they open an office after going through the trouble of getting a license? There are two reasons. First, the number of people buying and selling houses itself has decreased. As loans were tightened to curb housing prices. Everyone stopped trading, saying they would just wait and see. Without transactions, there is no money for agents to earn. Second, direct transactions that skip agents have increased. You know the secondhand marketplace app Karrot, right? People are now trading real estate directly on there, too. In 2022, the annual figure was about 7,000, but in 2024, it exceeded 34,000 in just seven months. With customers decreasing and the remaining ones moving to apps, it's hard to justify opening an office. We have a separate video on our channel that covers the story of real estate agents in more detail. I will leave the link in the pinned comment. At least real estate agents have the excuse that houses aren't selling. However, there is a place that is hiring fewer people despite earning the most money in history. 3rd place is bank employees. Do you know how much profit the four major commercial banks earned last year ? It’s a whopping 13.9 trillion won, an all-time high. Since they raked in so much money, you would naturally think they had a bonus feast and hired a massive number of people. But surprisingly, the reality shown by the statistics is the exact opposite. Over the past five years, the four major banks hired 7,517 new employees, but 9,120 people packed their bags through voluntary retirement. That means 1,600 more people left as if being pushed out than the number of new hires. In the first half of this year, Woori Bank even completely shut its doors to open recruitment. Why on earth is such a contradictory situation happening? The reason lies in the smartphones in our hands. We live in a world where deposits and loans can be handled with a few taps without ever needing to visit a bank. They reduced branches and naturally reduced hiring as well. It is a bitter reality that banks, once synonymous with lifelong employment, have narrowed the door for newcomers and widened the door for retirement during their golden age of record profits. At least for the 3rd place, bank employees, the company decided the hiring scale themselves. Then what is this 2nd place, where people are put through the trouble of passing a state-determined quota only to find nowhere to work? Just seven years ago, this was a profession where accounting firms offered signing bonuses to scout new passers, but now, three out of four cannot find a place to work. It is 2nd place, Certified Public Accountant. It is the very profession I mentioned at the beginning. Passing the exam doesn't mean an accountant can start working immediately. Doctors also start as interns at hospitals after passing the national exam, right? Accountants must also complete over a year of training at a firm to become full-fledged accountants. But last year, out of 1,200 successful candidates, only 338 found a place to train by late October. In medical terms, it’s as if three out of four people couldn't find a hospital to intern at after passing the national board exam. Last December, accountants even gathered in front of the Government Complex in Seoul every Monday morning to demand a reduction in the number of people selected. Why did it end up like this? Is it because accounting firms have become smaller? On the contrary, they have grown. The largest accounting firm in the country saw the number of its accountants grow from 2,046 to 2,512 over the past three years. However, during the same period, the number of entry-level trainees dropped from 383 to 260. The company grew, but the number of entry-level positions shrank. The industry points to AI as one of the key reasons. AI is now taking over a significant portion of the data organization and basic analysis tasks that new hires used to learn on the job. Looking at the recruitment ads from major overseas accounting firms last year, there were more than twice as many openings for AI experts as for audit staff. A decline in the overall workload for accounting firms also played a part. There is a country that went through this first. It’s Japan. Japan also faced a backlog of qualified accountants with nowhere to go. They eventually solved it by reducing the number of passers, but then the number of people wanting to take the exam plummeted. This was presented by researchers from the Korean Accounting Association at a seminar last year. Here, the number of successful candidates this year was 150 , down by 10 from last year. When you narrow the door, the people standing in front of it are the first to disappear. You pass the exam, but there are no positions to learn the ropes. Accountancy was a test that was difficult even to pass. Now, only one thing remains. Conversely, it’s a job where people were told a path would open up after just a few months of learning. The number one spot goes to entry-level developers. Do you remember 2021? We even saw phrases like "the war for developers." Game companies scrambled to raise starting salaries, with some offering as much as 60 million won. Stories were everywhere about liberal arts majors becoming developers after attending coding bootcamps for a few months. Back then, if your child or niece had quit their job to learn coding, you probably wouldn't have stopped them. It looked like the most certain path. What is it like now, after five years? These days, if you tell a computer in plain language to create a certain program, AI writes the code for you in a stream. The CEO of Google revealed this personally in April of this year. He said that AI now writes three out of every four new lines of code at Google. I don’t know a single line of code, but I use AI to create programs I need for video production just by giving it verbal instructions. In the meantime, entry-level positions have noticeably shrunk. The number of software developers in their 20s enrolled in employment insurance fell by one in ten over the past year. So, does that mean we no longer need developers? That is not the case. According to an analysis by the Bank of Korea, even in sectors heavily affected by AI, jobs for those in their 50s have increased by over 170,000. Experienced professionals work faster with AI by their side. AI has taken over the easy tasks that new hires used to learn first. To put it in terms of a restaurant, the head chef remains, but machines have filled the spots of the youngest staff who used to learn by washing dishes and prepping ingredients. Developers are still needed. Companies just have less reason to train new hires from scratch. We've looked at the jobs facing a crisis, from 7th to 1st place. Did you notice the chilling commonality running through all seven of these jobs? It’s not that the jobs themselves disappeared, but that the entry-level positions were the first to go. Let's re-examine one by one why only the junior roles are disappearing. For the 7 th-place designer, AI has taken over the tasks of the junior who printed drafts and edited photos. For the 6 th-place voice actor, AI voices have replaced the mall announcements and ad samples used for entry-level experience . For the 5 th-place translator, the number of working professionals actually rose, but one in three new job openings disappeared. For the 4 th-place real estate agent, there are far fewer new openings than there are existing offices. The 3 rd-place bank teller saw the biggest earnings ever, yet they cut back on new hiring first. That’s because mobile apps have taken over the counter work that new hires used to do. For the 2 nd-place accountant, even after passing the exam , they couldn't find a trainee position to learn the ropes. For the 1 st-place developer, AI now writes the simple code that newcomers used to handle. In all seven jobs, the people at the top remain, but the entry-level spots at the bottom are empty. So why specifically the junior positions? If you think about what juniors did, the answer becomes clear. Printing drafts, reading set lines, balancing numbers, and writing simple code—it's all work done by following set instructions. Come to think of it, companies were essentially paying newcomers to practice while doing these simple tasks . They needed to master these routine tasks before taking on real work that requires judgment and responsibility. But now, the landscape has completely shifted. AI handles tasks according to set rules much faster and cheaper than humans. For companies, the reason to spend time and money training new recruits has vanished. It’s not just machines taking jobs; the era of getting paid to learn on the job has ended. This is the real reason why entry-level roles are disappearing across these seven professions. But don't worry too much. The jobs themselves aren't disappearing entirely . It’s not as if all the job cuts are solely due to AI. There’s also the trend of correcting over-hiring during the pandemic, and some jobs, like real estate agents, were affected more by market conditions than AI. Let’s look at the 2 nd-place accountant again. AI took over the tasks of balancing numbers and organizing data. However, the final judgment—verifying those numbers and deciding what fits the company—is still a human role. Because someone needs to be there to tell AI when it gives a wrong answer. So, what will disappear are accountants who only do work that AI can do. Accountants who review the answers provided by AI and determine if they are accurate and appropriate will still be needed. An analysis by the Bank of Korea says the same thing. In sectors where AI completely replaces humans, youth employment has decreased significantly. However, in sectors where humans use AI as a tool, that trend was not as distinct. Even within the same profession, it splits between those who only do what AI can do and those who evaluate the results produced by AI. Then, in this changing world, what should we do right now? I recommend you try just one thing today. Type the name of your profession along with "AI" in the YouTube search bar. Like "Accountant AI,""Hair salon AI," or "Restaurant AI." These days, there are countless videos that show you how to use AI for every job. Even if you just follow one, you will be one step ahead—on the side of those who wield AI, rather than those whose jobs are taken by it. You must put in at least this much effort to not fall behind in a changing world. The same goes for when you advise your children or grandchildren. Rather than where they get hired, ask them first what they will be doing there. If it is work that follows a set process, AI can take it over first. If it is work where a person makes judgments and uses AI as a tool, the door is still open. This is exactly the standard I mentioned at the beginning. The era has come where a stable job is no longer stable. Instead , it is an era where those who keep learning and challenging themselves become the most secure, while those who stand still get left behind. So, rather than thinking you'll be safe just by entering a good company, I hope you prepare in advance according to the changing flow of jobs.
