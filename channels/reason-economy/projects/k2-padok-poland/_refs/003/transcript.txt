On September 18th, sirens suddenly rang out across eastern Poland. Explosions were even heard just a few meters from the Polish border. People there were even saying things like this. Whether we like it or not, this is already our war. On September 17th, local time, Russia attacked western Ukraine with drones, one of which approached the Ukrainian town of Yavoriv, just 4km from the Polish border, and exploded. Not long ago, a Russian drone also flew over the Ukrainian train station in Yavoriv, only 2km from the Polish border. This is the very rail line that a train carrying former British Prime Minister Boris Johnson, former Swedish Prime Minister Carl Bildt, and other European security officials had passed through. Now, the distance of the war that Poland feels has shrunk from hundreds of kilometers to just 2km or 4km. And right around that time, Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk made an even more tense statement. He stated that Poland is preparing for various scenarios, including war with Russia, and that the most important task now is to make the nation ready to respond to any situation. Yet, in the midst of this situation, another massive force appeared at a Polish port. It was the K2 tanks that flew in from South Korea. The moment sirens were ringing in eastern Poland and explosions were heard across the border, more K2 tanks arrived from Korea. On the 16th, a massive total of 18 K2 tanks arrived in Poland all at once. These tanks are scheduled to be deployed to the armored cavalry brigades under the mechanized division responsible for defending eastern Poland. But what’s truly surprising isn't just the fact that 18 more were added. It is the speed of delivery. Poland has already received all 180 K2 tanks ordered in the first contract. And the volume for the second contract, signed last August, has also begun to arrive in earnest. On August 25th, 28 K2 tanks from the second contract arrived in Poland. And just 22 days later, 18 more arrived. 28 tanks and 18 tanks—that's already 46 in just two shipments. In just over a year since the contract was signed, Korea has delivered them to Poland at an astonishing speed. Poland is effectively saying that a war with Russia has begun, and what they need in this situation is military power they can use right now. When these 18 tanks arrived again in just about 22 days, one Polish netizen said this: Great, now it should take four weeks instead of six to defeat Russia. Another netizen spoke as if they could hardly believe the speed of the delivery itself. They did it in just one year. That is really fast. The person who reports the news of Korean weapons, including the K2 tanks, arriving quickly is none other than Defense Minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz. Whenever he posts something like this, there is significant criticism pointing to his past actions. When the previous conservative government in Poland introduced a large number of South Korean weapons, the current defense minister and pro-EU factions harshly criticized it. Back when they were in the opposition, they claimed that the mass introduction of Korean weapons was a "chicken game" intended to build up the previous administration's achievements. They fired fierce criticisms day after day, claiming it would destroy Poland's domestic defense industry and place a massive burden on the national treasury. As soon as they took power, they even threatened to completely re-examine the arms contracts with South Korea. However, once they actually took power and faced not just the fierce threats from Russia but even a potential state of war, their stance shifted completely by 180 degrees. This is because while Western tanks were delayed indefinitely due to parts supply and production issues, only South Korea delivered cutting-edge tanks swiftly and accurately within the promised timeframe. Ultimately, the very defense minister who once shouted against buying Korean weapons was the first to announce the news on social media and brag about the arrival of 18 additional K2 tanks to the 16th Mechanized Division, the frontline unit defending the border near the Russian territory of Kaliningrad. People are saying that the opposition party, which was busy disparaging them, became the world's most ardent fan of Korean weapons the moment they took power. The reaction from the local military community and the Polish public is also explosive. Even the politicians who previously criticized the deals had no choice but to bow down before the incredible delivery speed and performance of Korean weapons, admitting that the NATO eastern front would have been breached if they had waited for German or American tanks. Now, people are saying, "What would we have done if we hadn't chosen Korean weapons?" As the security crisis in Europe intensifies, the reason why Poland is bringing in Korean weapons at such a rapid pace is becoming increasingly clear. Recently, however, a video released in Poland has been catching people's attention. From a distance, a K2 tank was moving at a high speed, changed direction, and smoothly moved on. It is easy to imagine a tank weighing over 50 tons swaying heavily or significantly reducing speed when turning at that level of speed. However, the K2 tank maintained its speed and navigated the corner smoothly, as if it were gliding over the road. The person who filmed this video is Mariusz Cielma, the editor-in-chief of a Polish military magazine. He said that he didn't realize it at the time of filming, but after reviewing the footage, he found the K 2's movement quite impressive. He used a specific expression to describe it. "The K2 moves as if it were flowing around the corner." The K2 tanks in the video are the ones the Polish military has already deployed and is currently operating in actual training exercises. Poland has already received K2 tanks, operating them for years and experiencing their pros and cons firsthand, with new tanks arriving and operational experience steadily accumulating. This has drawn particular attention lately, as tensions continue to rise along Poland's eastern border. What Poland needs is not weapons that will arrive in a few years, but force that can be deployed and utilized immediately. As the threat from Russia grows, countries that chose German Leopard tanks over K2s are now feeling regret. This includes nations like Norway, the Czech Republic, Lithuania, and Croatia, which are currently facing imminent threats from Russia. Amidst this, a significant announcement from South Korea is beginning to gain attention. Recently, the issue of tank defense against drones has been frequently raised in Poland and across Europe. In particular, the landscape of the battlefield has changed completely since the war in Ukraine. We repeatedly see scenes of inexpensive FPV drones flying over multi-million dollar tanks to strike their tops, or drones spotting tank locations to relay coordinates to artillery and other weapon systems. This problem is no exception for the K2 tanks currently operated by Poland. The South Korean Defense Acquisition Program Administration recently made an official announcement regarding a massive performance upgrade project for the K2 tank. With a total budget of 3.448 trillion won, the project is set to begin as early as 2028. However, what’s notable about this project is that it goes beyond simple replacements of communication or electronic equipment. South Korea is effectively attempting to evolve the K2 into a tank tailored for the modern battlefield. The key is survivability, incorporating Active Protection Systems (APS) to counter incoming anti-tank rockets and missiles, as well as jammers to thwart drones and improvised explosive threats . It also introduces a Remote Controlled Weapon Station (RCWS), allowing crew members to engage nearby threats without exposing themselves outside the vehicle. Since the war in Ukraine, suicide drones and FPV drones have emerged as some of the most fearsome enemies of modern tanks. As we keep seeing multi-million dollar tanks attacked by cheap drones, the competitiveness of modern tanks can no longer be judged solely by armor thickness or gun power. How quickly a tank can detect incoming drones, how effectively it can neutralize them, and whether it can survive to the end— that has become the new measure of tank competitiveness. The Defense Acquisition Program Administration stated that this upgrade will enhance the K 2's ability to counter new battlefield threats, including anti-tank rockets, missiles, and suicide drones. In short, they are turning the K2 into a tank for the age of drones. In Korea, seeing the enhanced defensive capabilities of the K2PL requested by Poland, some wondered if the Polish K2 might be better than the Korean military's K2, or if we should also upgrade to the K2PL level. But Korea wasn't just following the K2PL; it was planning something much bigger. The plan is to extensively upgrade the K2 tanks held by the Korean military to suit future battlefield environments and use the new defensive technologies secured in the process to boost K 2's export competitiveness. In other words, it’s not just about creating special equipment for the Korean military. Korea can further expand options for the export-type K2, allowing future buyers to choose enhanced defensive packages, including active protection systems and drone-countering equipment. And the important thing is that it’s not just about newly produced K2s. The strength of the K2 platform is that it can combine various types of equipment and improve performance in stages according to the customer's needs. Therefore, countries that have already introduced the K2 can also consider adding these defensive systems during future upgrade processes as needed. This was said to have significant implications for K2 exports as well. Because the contract doesn’t end with the initial sale; it allows for additional upgrade projects as new threats emerge over the decades. It’s not just selling a K2 tank, but exporting the K2 platform itself and the subsequent upgrade market along with it. And this is exactly what will be another powerful weapon for the K2 in the European tank market moving forward. On September 5th, the international military documentary channel "Mega Projects" uploaded a video about the K2 tank, claiming South Korea has developed a tank with mobility that surpasses the Abrams and Leopard 2. While listing the K2 tank's strengths, it highlighted it as a " terrain-conquering tank" with overwhelming specs that even the Abrams would envy. Various comments were left on this video, and the one with the most likes was written by a Polish person. The comment was very short. " Glad we bought it." That was the end of it.
