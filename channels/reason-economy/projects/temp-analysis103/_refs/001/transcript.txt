The instrument cluster is an option. How would you feel if a dealer said this to you when you went to check out a new car? The screen in front of the driver that showed the speed and remaining fuel—a feature that’s never been missing since we first started driving—is now something you have to pay extra for. But did you know this isn't a joke, but actually happening at Hyundai dealerships right now? This is about the new Avante released this summer. If you buy the cheapest Modern trim, the space in front of the driver is completely empty . To check your speed, you have to turn your head to the right and look at the large center screen. If you want the instrument cluster in front, they say you have to pay 350,000 won more. What’s even more ridiculous is that the previous model came with the cluster as a standard feature, even in the cheapest trim. They took it out, only to sell it back to you for a price . Hyundai’s stance is as follows. " The speed is displayed on the center screen, so what's the problem?""Isn't it fine if only those who want it choose it?" But what if you have to turn your head just to see how fast you’re driving in a new car you paid 23.98 million won for? Is that really a choice made for the customer? There is one more strange figure. The Avante set an all-time record for the model on its first day of pre-orders, with 11,094 units sold. But over half of those contracts—52%—were for the top-tier trim. That’s a car that costs 7.5 million won more than the cheapest trim . By taking out the instrument cluster, they pushed people to upgrade on their own. Since they’ve been doing business so well, is Hyundai’s domestic sales performing strongly? It’s the exact opposite. From January to September of this year, the number of cars Hyundai sold domestically is 448,181. This is the lowest figure since 2008, when the global financial crisis hit, with 440,763 units. Everyone says it’s because of the strikes. Or because the economy is bad. That’s what people say. That’s correct. Those are reasons, too. But that is merely the result. The reason why they hit rock bottom at home despite launching a car that saw a rush of contracts on day one lies in how Hyundai views domestic customers like us. The cheapest Avante gasoline Modern trim is 23.98 million won. The 10.25-inch center screen comes as standard. However, the 10.25-inch slim display in front of the driver— what we call the instrument cluster— requires an extra 350,000 won to install. The price of the car with the instrument cluster becomes 24.33 million won. Many of you are probably thinking this right now. "Then if I buy one trim level higher, the instrument cluster would obviously be included, right?" No, it isn't. This is where the real business strategy begins. In the 27.71 million won Premium trim, you cannot choose the instrument cluster on its own. You have to buy the entire 830,000 won package that bundles the 14.6-inch center screen and the instrument cluster together. That brings the car's price to 28.54 million won. Let's summarize it like this. The Premium trim is 3.73 million won more expensive than the Modern. It adds heated front seats, ventilated seats, and better air conditioning. However, the instrument cluster is still missing . You paid 3.73 million won more, but the space in front of the driver remains empty. The only trim that comes with the instrument cluster from the start is the top-tier Inspiration, which starts at 31.52 million won. Between gasoline and hybrid models, there are six base trims, but only two of them include the driver's display as standard. The instrument cluster has become an optional item, like a sunroof or premium audio. The screen you look at most in a car, that is. Do you know how much a fully-optioned Avante Hybrid costs, with the sunroof and premium audio added? It's 42.34 million won, before applying tax benefits. It’s an Avante. It's the car we used to call the "people's car." The car young professionals buy as their first vehicle. The car people drive after sending their kids to college and downsizing. That car, fully optioned, is 42.34 million won. Keep this number in mind. It will come up again later. But the part that’s truly infuriating here isn't the price. If you look at the price list of the previous generation Avante, the cheapest Smart trim was 20.62 million won, and even that included a 4.2-inch color screen instrument cluster as a standard feature. What was included in a 20.62 million won car was removed from a 23.98 million won car. The price of the cheapest Avante rose by 3.36 million won in the meantime. That's a 16.3% increase. Some of you might be thinking this: "But surely it didn't go up that much if you compare the same Modern trims." That's correct. Comparing Modern trims, it only went up by 100,000 won, from 23.88 million to 23.98 million won. However, the previous generation's Modern trim also had the 4.2-inch cluster as standard. They took away the cluster while charging 100,000 won more. They made it look like the price didn't increase while reducing what the customer gets. This is what the industry calls " de-contenting." In our terms, it's " stripping the core." So, are all Hyundai cars like this? That’s not the case, which makes this story interesting. Take the new Tucson, for which the same company released a price list this month. The cheapest trim, the gasoline 1.6 Turbo, is 31.40 million won, and it comes with a 9.9-inch instrument cluster as standard. It also includes a surround-view monitor and front/rear parking collision-avoidance assist. It's the same company, the same time, and the same parts, but it's standard on the cheapest Tucson trim, while it's 350,000 won for the Avante. The argument that it's due to production costs falters right here. Of course, that doesn't mean the Tucson doesn't have its own hurdles. To add the ventilated front seats and upgraded air conditioning missing from the base model, you have to choose the 790,000 won Comfort Package. If you add that, the price becomes 32.19 million won. And if you add four-wheel drive and a sunroof to the top-tier Tucson Hybrid, it reaches 50.95 million won before tax benefits. We've entered an era where the Tucson exceeds 50 million won. Now, these are numbers anyone can verify at a showroom . So why does Hyundai Motor do this? We should also hear the side that claims they are being unfairly criticized. While researching the data, there was a point I found quite plausible. The first explanation is the cost of software. The new Avante features a new infotainment system called ccOS Connect . It even includes the ability to update vehicle software wirelessly. On an anonymous employee community, someone identified as a Hyundai employee posted that the usage fees for ccOS Connect have driven up new car prices. In fact, 42dot, the subsidiary that creates this software, had an operating loss of 349.8 billion won last year. They spent 377.5 billion won in operating expenses but only brought in 27.7 billion won. They spent 3.6 times more than they earned. Where would they make up for that deficit? Ultimately, the argument is that it comes from new car prices. The second explanation is that this year's circumstances were truly poor. Labor negotiations were prolonged, halting production; a parts supplier fire stopped car manufacturing; and they were hit with tariffs. Second-quarter operating profit was 2.859 trillion won , which increased from the first quarter but fell 20.8%compared to the same quarter last year. Revenue was a record-breaking 49.2153 trillion won for the quarter, but profits had shrunk . It means the company was in an urgent position to make money. The third explanation is product value. Hyundai Motor newly created a recommended combination section called "Best Pick" on its price lists. This was a response to complaints that options were too complicated. When unveiling the new Avante, the president said this: Applying the latest technology and product value to entry-level models shows how much they value customers choosing Hyundai for the first time. Even after the price controversy grew, they stated that prices are determined by market and competitive conditions, and they would take action if necessary . There is also one industry logic. Since modern cars are software-driven machines, it is becoming standard practice to pay for features later to unlock them. In fact, the new Palisade model included a subscription-based " Welcome Light" option, which sparked controversy. So, it's a system where the car's hardware is already installed , but you have to pay extra to use the features. Following this logic, the 350,000 won for the instrument cluster is also just a sign of the times. Listening this far, you might think it makes some sense, right? I thought so, too. But these explanations fall apart one by one when you apply a few numbers . First, let's look at the claim about software costs. If they had to remove it because the screen was expensive, the first thing they should have cut is the large center display. The center screen for the Modern trim is 12.9 inches. The instrument cluster in front of the driver is 9.9 inches, which is smaller. They removed the smaller one and kept the larger one as standard. If they really wanted to save on costs, they would have done the opposite. The reason they removed the small screen wasn't the cost, but because it's the most effective way to force people to upgrade to a higher trim. This isn't just us saying this. U.S. automotive media outlet Motor1 commented that making people pay extra to see essential information like speed is picking the wrong place to save a few pennies. Carscoops pointed out that making such a small screen an optional item is inappropriate, under the headline that Hyundai should know better. Even reporters abroad saw that this is not a money issue. What about the second explanation, that they were desperate because things are bad this year? If so, the company's situation should have improved as much as they squeezed the customers 'wallets. Hyundai Motor's own publicly stated goal this January was an operating margin of 6.3%to 7.3%. However, the actual operating margin for the first half of the year is 5.6%. It’s 0.7 percentage points lower than the bottom end of their own target. First-half operating profit was 5.3656 trillion won, down 25.8%from a year ago. They even sold the instrument cluster separately, yet the profit margin fell. This means it’s not a temporary measure due to urgency, but a direction they intend to push forward with regardless. The third claim, that it's an option for the customer, is refuted by the price list itself. The recommended "Best Pick" combination looks good, right? But unlike bundled packages for other models, this configuration offers no discount benefits. It doesn't provide a discount by bundling; it just reduces the hassle of choosing. They take the full amount of money. And the company's intended direction is already public. At an investor briefing last August, Hyundai presented a target operating margin of over 9%by 2030. They stated they would lower the cost-of-sales ratio by 3 percentage points more than originally planned. Cut costs and raise margins. Then who bears the brunt of those cut costs? On one side, it's the small and medium-sized suppliers who make the parts, and on the other, it's us paying extra to fill in the equipment missing from the base model. When you reach the end, the picture looks a bit different. The 350,000 won for the instrument cluster isn't a mistake, a cost issue, or a phase of transition. It’s a designed structure. Now, let’s uncover the real reasons one by one. There are three layers. The first layer is the most cunning. The first layer is the trim ladder. If you look at what actually happened after removing the instrument cluster, you’ll understand why it’s so cunning. On the first day of Elantra (Avante) pre-orders, 52%of people chose the top-tier Inspiration trim. Modern and Premium trims were at 24%each. Only one in four people bought the cheapest trim, while one in two bought the most expensive one. That means half the people who came to see a 23.98 million won car left after signing for a 31.52 million won car. That’s 7.5 million won per person. It means the actual selling price of the car advertised as starting at 23.98 million won is 31.52 million won. The reason this happens is because the middle is a trap. If you want to add just the instrument cluster, you have to buy an integrated package costing between 830,000 and 960,000 won. To get the proper look, meaning the lights found on higher trims, you have to move up to the 27.71 million won mid-range trim and then add a 1.4 million won exterior package. In the end, someone who just wants the instrument cluster and the lights ends up spending anywhere from 4 million to 5.4 million won more. Anyone who crunches the numbers up to that point thinks like this: "For this price, I might as well just go for the top model." Making you think that is the goal of this price list. What’s even more surprising is that Hyundai doesn't even hide this ladder. CEO Munoz explained the role of the Elantra like this. You can start with an Elantra and move up to a Sonata , Tucson, Santa Fe, Palisade, and eventually a Genesis? It means the Elantra is the entry point. The design is for you to come in and move up one step at a time. While we think we’re choosing a car, they are calculating which step we will climb to. That ladder also spans across different car models. If you fully load the options on a Tucson, it costs 49.95 million won . A Palisade 9-seater Exclusive, which is one class up, is 50.77 million won. The difference is just 820,000 won. Once you get to this point, almost everyone thinks the same thing. " Let’s pay 820,000 won more and drive a bigger car." It’s a structure where anyone fully loading a Tucson eventually becomes a Palisade customer. This isn't just a one-time occurrence. The same point was made back in 2020. Back then, the base price of the cheapest gasoline Modern trim was 24.35 million won. However, when you added options like lights, safety features, and navigation, the total came to 28.37 million won. But the Premium trim, which included those options as standard, was 26.41 million won. Adding options to the mid-level trim led to the strange phenomenon of the price exceeding the next trim up. So, back then, the best-selling model in actual contracts was the top-tier trim priced at 31.55 million won. They are using the same method, proven effective six years ago, this year by even manipulating the instrument cluster. The same tactic is used in other vehicles as well. As the Sorento moved into the 2026 model year, the digital center mirror and fingerprint authentication features could no longer be selected separately and were moved to the Smart Connect package. To put it in the words of a news report, the basic configuration has been somewhat reduced. Kia also caused controversy by bundling the auto-wiper rain sensor and built-in dashcam into a single package. It's a situation where you end up paying for a dashcam just for one wiper sensor. Do you know what people say at the end of this ladder? The new Sonata Hybrid Black Edition has climbed to 42.12 million won before tax benefits. This led to reactions from consumers saying that with that budget, they would rather consider the Grandeur, which is one class higher. People who came for a Sonata look at the Grandeur, those who came for a Tucson look at the Palisade, and those who came for an Avante end up signing for an Inspiration trim. From the company's perspective, this is a success. But from our perspective, what is it? The cars we could afford with our initial budget have disappeared. Let's move on to the second layer. It is whether this business actually made money. It did. It did very well. Hyundai Motor's average domestic passenger car sales price reached 57.25 million won as of the first half of this year. That’s a 3.5%increase from the 55.3 million won in the first half of 2025. It’s quite different from the domestic car prices we usually think of, isn't it? That is what the average is. The median value has climbed all the way up there. During the same period, Hyundai's global production was 1,838,476 units. That is a 6.7%decrease from the 1,971,255 units in the first half of 2025. They made less and sold less, yet the money earned per vehicle went up. The company's explanation for this is important. The shift in sales composition toward SUVs and high-end trims for electrified models. The phrase "high-end trim focus" is the core of today's story. The reason for removing the instrument cluster is all contained in that one line. If you look at one more number, it becomes clear that this is no coincidence. Hyundai and Kia's high-profit trim sales account for 68.5%. By the same standard , GM is at 65.1%, Toyota is at 63.0%, and Volkswagen is at 55.1%. It's 1%. This means Hyundai and Kia are the companies that best sell expensive trims in the world. The change in sales composition toward SUVs and high-end trims was the key factor in driving up the average price. Kia's average sales price rose 11.7%in one year to 29 million won, hitting an all-time high. Thanks to that, Kia's Q1 operating profit margin was 8.8%, and Hyundai's was 6.4%. It was 4%. You should notice something strange here. The revenue per unit has risen, and the share of expensive trims is the highest in the world. Yet, the first-half operating profit margin stood at just 5.6%. Domestic sales during the same period dropped by 16.3%. They succeeded in getting more from each customer, but the number of customers is shrinking. This is the end result of the "option business." While they were getting 7.5 million won more from one person, several others quietly disappeared. The third layer hurts the most. It's a story about how Hyundai has treated its domestic customers. Let’s make one thing clear before we start. There’s a long-standing rumor on the internet that domestic cars use cheaper steel plates. That is not true. Hyundai stated that since late 2006, they have applied over 70%galvanized steel to domestic cars, just like their exports, and since 2011, it has been applied to all passenger car models. We also couldn't find any evidence to refute that claim this time. So, I won't say that the car bodies are different. But there are documented differences in other areas, not just the steel plates. First, the airbags. Looking at 2013 data, only the Genesis and Equus— about 5%of domestic vehicles—had advanced airbags, while the rest were equipped with depowered airbags. A depowered airbag has its deployment force reduced by 20 to 30%, a type that was banned by law in the US due to safety concerns. They installed legally required airbags in cars sold in the US , but put something else in cars for the domestic market. In 2015, as the controversy grew, Hyundai said they were actively considering equipping all models with advanced airbags in the mid-to-long term. They changed it because they were pressured. Back then, airbags were even sold as options. For the domestic Sonata, you had to bundle 26 options to get six airbags, which added 5.79 million won to the cost. For the Kia Soul, you had to pay an extra 2.12 million won. Can you see where the current practice of selling dashboard clusters for 350,000 won comes from? It’s not a new tactic. Only the items have changed. There are also areas you can't see. In 2016, the bumper rail structure for the Tucson's collision reinforcement was different between domestic and US models. The car built for US testing had extended left and right edges on the reinforcement, but the domestic model lacked that extension. Hyundai countered that they don't intentionally discriminate, that it was to meet US crash tests, and that all manufacturers respond in the same way. The company has a point, too. But that same year, the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport delayed adopting those testing standards domestically. Experts evaluated that it only appears the government is showing favoritism toward domestic automakers. We are the only ones who decided not to take that test. It has been pointed out twice in the National Assembly as well. In the 2016 parliamentary audit, data showed that while there were 52 recalls in North America, only 24 of those, or 46.1%, were also recalled domestically. Even those 24 domestic recalls were 100 %slower than in the U.S., taking anywhere from one week to over two and a half months longer. In terms of the number of recalled vehicles, there were 1,207,592 in Korea and 4,045,637 in North America. In 2017, the warranty period for the Theta II engine became an issue. While they offered a lifetime warranty in the U.S., they only applied a 10-year, 190,000 km warranty in Korea . The Hyundai Motor president who appeared as a witness said there were aspects that did not meet the market's expectations. Pricing follows the same pattern. At the same time, domestically, the 1.6 gasoline Smart trim price rose by 190,000 won and the Modern trim by 530,000 won with the release of the new model year. It was exactly the same in 2017. The new Avante sold in the U.S. started at $ 17,150, which is $ 100 lower than the previous base price for the same trim. Domestic prices increased every time a new car was released. While they were cutting prices on one side, they were raising them on the other. There is only one reason why this happens. It is because they cannot sell in the U.S. without discounts, while they still sold in Korea even when raising prices. At least, that has been the case until now. And the trim structure is not complicated. When Tesla released the entry-level Model Y Standard, they lowered the price by 7 million won while removing several premium options. If you remove options, you lower the price. This is a calculation that customers can understand. Our side removed options and raised the price. China's BYD is even more aggressive. The domestic manufacturer's suggested retail price for the base Atto 3 trim is 31.5 million won. It comes standard with blind-spot collision assist, safe exit assist, and smart cruise control. It comes standard with a 3D surround view monitor, panoramic sunroof, and over-the-air software updates. While we’re talking about paying 350,000 won for an upgrade in the Avante, they’ve come in with the sunroof as a standard feature. The Atto 3 sold 543 units in its first month in Korea, taking the top spot among imported electric vehicles. And this price point didn't just happen by accident. The domestic price of 31.5 million won for the base Atto 3 trim is about 45% cheaper than it is in Europe. They’ve priced it lower than their own home market to break into Korea. Our companies are raising prices at home, while their company cut prices to enter our home turf. In this fight, isn't it obvious where customers will go, even without a calculator? You could brush this off by saying it's a Chinese car or blaming subsidies. But there is a survey that directly asked customers why they chose imported cars. 37.2%of people cited safety, quality, and reliability as the reason for choosing an import over a domestic car. Design and exterior were 33.9%, and value for the price was 29.0%. It’s been labeled a " people's car," but it's no longer a car that individuals are actually buying. Does this feel like someone else's company's problem to you? Now let’s look at how that bill eventually flies back to us. The first bill arrives when you try to resell the car. Many of you think the money spent on options will come back in the resale value, right? According to analysis by used car platforms, the option that best prevents depreciation is the sunroof, and that only adds about 1.5%to a maximum of 2%of the vehicle's price. Navigation is about 1%, head-up display is also about 1%, and lane keeping assist is 0.5%to 1%. Industry analysis clearly states this as well. There is no guarantee that the price of a base vehicle with added options will be valued exactly that much higher in the used car market. If you spent 42.34 million won on a fully-optioned Avante, most of that difference simply vanishes . The second bill arrives when you have an accident. Analysis shows that repair costs for cars with advanced safety equipment can increase by up to 37.6% after an accident. Just replacing the windshield and recalibrating the camera costs between 500,000 and 1 million won . If the radar sensor or camera module inside the bumper is completely shattered, it's 3 million to over 8 million won. The point is that modern front bumpers are closer to precision electronic devices than the simple plastic chunks of the past. That money eventually returns to us in the form of insurance premiums. In 2024, the cost of just replacing and repairing bumpers for domestic and imported cars was 1.3578 trillion won. That is 17%of the total 7.8423 trillion won in car insurance repair costs. There was a standard created in 2017 to repair small scratches rather than replace them, but the application rate in 2024 was only 4%. According to researcher analysis, reducing bumper replacement cases by just 30%could lower total repair costs by 6.4%. The cost of optional sensors becomes millions of won in a single accident, which then piles up into our insurance bills. The price of an option doesn't end with the amount written on the contract. The third bill arrives with your next car. A person who goes to look at an Avante and signs for an Inspiration trim looks at a Sonata next. Someone who bought the top-tier Sonata looks at a Grandeur next. It is exactly the migration path that the CEO mentioned. It’s easy to think that our cars are getting better because our standard of living is rising, but we didn't build that ladder . The price tag did. The fourth bill is the right to choose. In the past, there was the option of a "bare-bones" car. When money was tight, you could buy a car with only the bare essentials. But now, the 23.98 million won Avante doesn't have a driver's instrument cluster, and the space where the 20.62 million won Avante used to be is now empty. The freedom to choose a cheap car has disappeared. That is the most expensive bill of all. But doesn't one thing make you curious here? Why did Hyundai Motor have to go this far, and why now? Domestically, it rose 3.5%over the same period. It means the strategy of raising prices abroad has worked. The reason a foreigner was appointed CEO of Hyundai Motor for the first time was also to improve sales and profitability. By managing dealers in the U.S., they were able to sell even better despite raising prices. So, the price tags we are seeing now are the result of bringing the successful U.S. strategy home. The problem is that the market is different. To U.S. customers, Hyundai is one of many options, but to Korean customers, Hyundai has long been almost the only option. They used that trust as capital to raise prices. But that capital began to disappear this year along with the 89,148 Tesla vehicles sold. KGM chose to focus on expanding their SUV and pickup truck lineup, such as the Musso, Torres, and EVX, by focusing on cost-effectiveness and functionality. Industry experts believe Renault Korea and KGM are aiming to expand domestic market share with a mass-market strategy based on reasonable prices and practicality. Kia also lifted its option restrictions when launching the K7 Premier in 2019. At the time, over 70%of all consumers selected the driver assistance option package. The Stinger included that equipment as standard even in lower trims starting with the 2020 model. So, shifting toward making features standard is certainly possible. It’s not that they can’t do it; they only don’t do it when they think it will sell anyway. The market is already providing the answer. On October 23, Hyundai Motor’s closing price was 295,500 won. Compared to the peak of 750,000 won on June 1, that is a 60.6% plunge. They say profit per unit increased by charging more for options, but the stock price dropped below half its peak in just four months. This year , foreign investors net sold 11.36 trillion won of Hyundai stock, while individuals net bought 12.36 trillion won. Individuals stepped in to buy what the experts sold off. If you are currently holding Hyundai stock, do not just brush these numbers aside. Domestic sales and stock prices move together. Still, there is one glimmer of hope. When customers turn their backs, companies change. The new Tucson , released after the dashboard controversy, included the digital cluster as standard from the cheapest trim, and equipment like the Surround View Monitor and Highway Driving Assist are now included from the base trim. Public discontent changed the price tag . It means that it's not like there is nothing we can do. Then what should we look at first when changing cars? What kind of object a car has become has also changed. One article summarized the transformation of mid-size sedans like this. In the past, the mid-size sedan was the quintessential family car, defined by its practicality and reasonable price, but has its nature shifted now into a product that showcases individual design tastes and personality? That is correct. But while the family car is becoming a lifestyle product, who is making cars that can be bought at a family car price? The starting price for a flagship Genesis model is 50 million won. The upper tiers are packed tightly, but the lower tiers are being erased one by one. The results are reflected directly in the numbers from this September. Domestic sales for the five major automakers in September totaled 99,843 units. That is a 19.8%decrease from the 124,515 units sold a year ago. While the five domestic companies lost 24,672 units, Tesla alone increased its sales by 3,303 units. BYD also rose to 4th place among imported car brands last month with 2,614 units. Losing over 20,000 units a month in our own backyard cannot be explained by the economy alone. It is not that the demand has disappeared, but that cars at an affordable price have vanished. So, I hope you remember these three things when you next change your car. First, forget the starting price shown in advertisements. Write down two or three features you absolutely need, and start by asking how much it costs with all of them included. For the Avante, just opting for the instrument cluster and lamps added between 4 million and 5.4 million won. That is the real price. Second, count how many items in a bundled package you won't actually use. There was actually a configuration where you had to pay for a dashcam just to get a wiper sensor. Even if it's labeled a "recommended combination," there is often no discount, so be sure to compare the bundle price with buying items separately. Third, just think for a moment about how much of that option's value you can recover when reselling the car. Even a sunroof only retains 1.5%to 2%of its value. Unless you plan to drive it for a long time, that money won't come back if you plan to trade it in after three years. Let's go back to the beginning. The instrument cluster is an option. This phrase was not just a story ending with an absurd price tag. It is a structure where they take out what used to be standard, sell it separately, bundle it into packages, and force customers to move up the ladder, charging millions of won more. Through that structure, the revenue per vehicle has been raised to 57.25 million won. However, domestic sales this year have actually dropped by 16.3%to 448,180 units. They succeeded in moving customers up the ladder, but failed to keep the customers. I will leave you with one line.
