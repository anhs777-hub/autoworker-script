Following the acquisition of U.S.-made High Mobility Artillery Rocket Systems, Croatia has now moved to purchase South Korea's Chunmoo multiple launch rocket system. The purchase is believed to be a move to bolster long-range strike capabilities and reduce reliance on U.S. munitions amid rising tensions in the Balkans due to Serbia's military buildup. According to defense industry sources on the 4th, Hanwha Aerospace is nearing a contract to supply Croatia with an integrated system including 18 Chunmoo launchers and guided missiles. The contract is valued at approximately 435.2 million euros and is reportedly expected to be finalized as early as next week. Defense Romania noted on the 7th that the primary reason for Croatia's decision to add the Chunmoo to its HIMARS fleet is the issue of U.S. missile supply. According to reports, the U.S. has halted the supply of 300 km-range missiles to allies due to weight concerns and does not authorize the export of 500 km-range missiles. The HIMARS contract signed by Croatia includes only 80 km-range rockets, making it difficult to secure the desired long-range strike capability with that deal alone. In response, Hanwha Aerospace presented an alternative to overcome these constraints. According to Defense Romania, through the contract with Hanwha Aerospace, Croatia will secure CTM-290 tactical ballistic missiles capable of striking targets over 300km away. Croatia’s focus on the Chunmoo is interpreted as a desire to secure additional launchers while acquiring South Korean munitions capable of engaging targets at greater distances. Behind Croatia's rush to secure long-range missile capabilities lies the influence of Serbia. Serbia has recently been strengthening its strike capabilities by purchasing Israeli PULS multiple launch rocket systems and Chinese missiles. Feeling the pressure of its neighbor's military buildup, Croatia aims to increase its deterrent by securing weapons that can reach deep into enemy territory. Fast delivery was also cited as a key strength of the Korean proposal. Hanwha Aerospace plans to deliver more than 85%of the systems by 2028. In a global climate of increasing arms demand, this allows Croatia to reinforce its long-range firepower within a relatively short period. Moreover, the Chunmoo boasts twice the firepower of the HIMARS. The Chunmoo is equipped with two modular containers that can hold a total of twelve 239mm missiles or two 300 km-range ballistic missiles, whereas HIMARS uses only one container holding six missiles. This flexibility allows for the combination of different calibers of ammunition on the same vehicle, enabling operations tailored to specific tactical situations. Croatian media reported that through this Chunmoo purchase, Croatia has reduced its reliance on American weapons and secured operational independence by upgrading its current equipment with platforms capable of fundamentally altering the security landscape of Southeast Europe.
