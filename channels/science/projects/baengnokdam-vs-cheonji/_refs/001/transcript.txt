The deepest point is about 384 meters. That's deep enough that even if you stood the 63 Building inside, there would still be over 100 meters of water above its roof. From the lake that originally had no fish, to the day a millennium ago when white ash fell like snow as far as Japan and drained all the water, to the true secrets hidden at the bottom of the lake. If you enjoy stories like this, please subscribe and like the video. Shall we descend into the sky lake? First, let’s look at what kind of place Heaven Lake is. Heaven Lake is a lake floating in the sky at an altitude of over 2,000 meters. That’s why its name, "Cheonji," literally means "Heavenly Pond." The bowl that cradles Heaven Lake is called a caldera . When underground magma erupts all at once, the mountain top collapses into the empty space left behind. That massive pit created by the collapse is the caldera. The rim of the Baekdusan caldera alone is about 20km in circumference. Rainwater and melted snow collected inside it, becoming Heaven Lake. In China, they call the same mountain "Changbaishan." That’s why most tourists going to see Heaven Lake climb up via the Chinese side. The surface area is about 9.2 square kilometers. It’s about three times the size of Yeouido, and walking around the perimeter takes over 14km. The water contained here is about 2 billion tons. That’s enough for every person in South Korea to have 40 tons each. The water of the lake is exceptionally blue because it is clear and deep. Deep water absorbs red light and reflects only the blue light back. The highest peak surrounding the lake is 2,744 meters. It’s the highest point on the Korean Peninsula. But in North Korea, they refer to the same peak as 2,750 meters. Where did those 6 meters go? The South uses the sea off Incheon as a baseline for altitude, while the North uses the sea off Wonsan, so the numbers for the same mountain differ. And a national border runs right through this lake. North Korea holds 54.5%and China holds 45.5%; it was a secret treaty signed in 1962, so we only learned the details in 1999. Even though it’s the same water, one side is North Korea and the other is China. Let’s start draining that water now. If you drain just 1 meter of water, there’s something that stops immediately. It is the "Dalmun," the gap between the northern peaks where water flows out. The water that flows out through the Dalmun falls as the 68-meter-high Changbai Waterfall and becomes China’s Songhua River. People often say that Heaven Lake is the source of the Yalu and Tumen Rivers. But the only path for water to exit above ground is Dalmun. Then where do the Amnok and Duman rivers begin? Water from Heaven Lake seeps underground and springs up here and there below the mountain. The two rivers start from those spring waters. The amount of water exiting through Dalmun annually is estimated at about 36 million tons. That is less than 2%of the total water in Heaven Lake. So even if the water level drops a little, the exit dries up. In fact, in the summer of 1978, the water flowing out through Dalmun stopped completely. Once the exit closes, Heaven Lake is just a giant bowl of water pooled in the sky. A bowl filled only by rainwater, snow, and water seeping from the ground. This time, let's drain 100 meters. Before that, Heaven Lake is frozen for more than half of the year. From October to June. It is covered by ice with an average thickness of 1.5 meters. Why does it stay frozen for so long? Above 2,000 meters in altitude, the temperature is over 10 degrees lower than at the coast . On top of that, fierce winds on the mountain peak are added. An ice thickness of 1.5 meters is chest-high. The water beneath is cold all year round, so originally not a single fish lived in this lake. The biggest reason is likely the waterfall. Hmm. Because fish living in the river below cannot swim up the 68-meter Changbai Waterfall . However, on July 30, 1960, North Korea released fish into this lake. Nine cherry salmon and 16 crucian carp. The result? The crucian carp couldn't endure it, and only the cherry salmon, which likes cold water, survived. Almost all the fish living in Heaven Lake now are descendants of those cherry salmon. In 2017, they also released 2,500 pond smelt. But it is said that only about 100 survived. Heaven Lake is that harsh a place for fish. And when it comes to Heaven Lake, the monster story is a must. Sightings by tourists on the Chinese side claiming to have seen a black object have appeared several times since the 2000s. But the North Korean scientists 'answer was underwhelming. They said the identity of the monster is likely a cherry salmon that has grown very large . To this day, not a single monster has been confirmed. >> Calling me a monster, how disappointing . Let's drain more water. If we drain 250 meters, the top of the 63 Building would finally emerge if we had placed it inside. But isn't it strange? We drained 250 meters, but the bottom still isn't visible. Heaven Lake is shaped like a deep bowl that drops steeply from the edges. How much water is left? Since the average depth is about 213m, even after subtracting 250m , there is still over 130m of water pooled in the deepest center. Looking at the exposed walls as the water recedes, various colored stripes appear . Gray lava, yellow pumice, red burnt soil—it is Mt. Baekdu’s diary, stacked layer by layer with every eruption. If you imagine standing at the bottom and looking up, you are surrounded by sheer cliffs on all sides . From the rim to the floor. The depth of this bowl is over 900m. Finally, the last of the water drains away. This is the bare floor of Lake Cheonji at 384m deep. Depending on the time of measurement, it is also called 373m. Is the floor a flat mud base? A 2018 survey that mapped the floor using sound revealed unexpected things. How can we map the underwater terrain using sound? When sound is fired from a boat, it hits the bottom and bounces back. We measure the depth by the time it takes to return, and by collecting this countless times, we draw a map of the floor. A small crater in the west, a round hill of hardened lava in the east , and traces of collapsed walls—all five features formed after a massive explosion. The round hill in the east formed when sticky lava couldn't flow far and swelled up and hardened right where it emerged. It is essentially a small volcano hidden underwater. Standing on the bare floor and looking up, the former water level is as high as the 63 Building. Above that, hundred-meter-tall cliffs surround the area. Four hot springs gush out along the water's edge. They are the first sign that the ground has not yet fully cooled. Then, when did the massive explosion that created this bowl happen ? Let's dig into the ground. Digging into the soil at the rim of Lake Cheonji reveals a layer of white volcanic ash and pumice about 3.5 m thick from the surface. It is a layer left behind by a great eruption a thousand years ago. The white stones in this layer are called pumice. Full of holes where gas escaped, they actually float on water despite being stones. In the winter of 946, Mt. Baekdu erupted. The volcanic column soared over 30km into the sky. That is three times the altitude at which airplanes fly. The volcanic ash and stones spewed out were several times more than that of Mount Vesuvius, which buried Pompeii in 79 AD . It was one of the greatest eruptions of the last 2,000 years. The eruption did not end just once. After spewing white ash, it erupted once more with gray ash of a different composition. Both times, the ash columns soared to 30 to 40km. The ash rode the wind and flew toward the east. It piled up 4 to 5cm in northern Japan, 1,000 km away, and was even found deep in the ice of Greenland. There is a record in a temple in Nara, Japan. On November 3, 946, white ash fell from the sky like snow. November in Nara was not yet the season for snow. That is why people likely found the white powder falling from the sky strange and recorded it. Scientists have narrowed down this date to within a few months. They counted 102 tree rings starting from the cosmic radiation marks left in 775, found in trees buried by the explosion. Tree rings grow one line per year. However, in 775, there was a sudden spike in cosmic radiation, leaving a distinct mark on that year's ring. By counting the rings outward from that mark, they could determine the year the tree died. At one time, there was a theory that this explosion caused the fall of Balhae. The idea was that the nation was buried in volcanic ash. The tree rings provided the answer. Balhae fell in 926. The explosion occurred 20 years later. The volcano merely scattered ash over a nation that had already disappeared. The truly surprising part comes next. As water filled the crater created by the explosion, one of its walls could not hold and burst. The amount of water that poured out was at least over 1 billion tons. That is about half of the water in Lake Cheonji today. The collapsing wall was eroded by 30 meters every hour. Why did the wall collapse? Immediately after the explosion, the crater wall was just piled-up volcanic ash and pumice, like a dirt mound. It ultimately could not withstand the weight of the rising water. The rushing water carved deep channels into the ground as it flowed down the valley. Those traces still remain in the valleys of Mount Baekdu today. What we are imagining today is something Lake Cheonji actually did over a thousand years ago. It drained itself. Let's dig deeper into the ground. Below the 946 layer, we find a yellow pumice layer that is nearly 50 meters thick. 3.5 meters versus 50 meters. The ancient eruption layer below is much thicker than the 946 layer we called the "Great Eruption." There is research suggesting that the crater of Cheonji has been growing little by little for tens of thousands of years. So, 946 was just one scene in the life of Mount Baekdu. It erupted countless times before that, and each time, the crater grew a little wider. If we go down further, we find black basalt. It is land formed by vast amounts of lava that flowed and hardened long ago, a massive lava plateau stretching 240km east-west and 400km north-south, reaching the Kaema Plateau. Near Mount Baekdu, this basalt layer is about 500 meters thick. It is a stone slab thicker than stacking two 63 Buildings on top of each other. The sequence is like this. First, lava flowed to create a wide plateau, then repeated explosions on top built up the mountain, and finally, the peak collapsed to form Cheonji. Mount Baekdu is not a mountain that formed all at once. It is a tower of fire that erupted and piled up countless times. >> So, the lake you live in is water collected in a bowl made by fire. >> Wait. Does that mean the fire is still underneath? >> Let's go deeper to find out. Now, let's head deep underground. By looking at seismic waves and electrical flow underground, we found layers where molten rock has collected beneath Mount Paektu. How do we look underground? Seismic waves slow down when passing through molten rock, and molten rock conducts electricity well. We measure these two things to draw a map of the underground. Some are shallow, a few kilometers deep, while others are 10 to 15 kilometers down. These are the magma chambers. A magma chamber isn't a large cave filled with sloshing lava. It’s more like a sponge, with molten magma seeping into the crevices of hot rock. Then, in 2002, the fire began to stir. Volcanic earthquakes, which averaged seven per month, jumped to 42, and in November 2003, there were 243 tremors. However, most of these were small quakes that people could barely feel. They were signals detected first by observation equipment. The ground also swelled. About 7cm over three years. The entire mountain rose as if taking a deep breath. The swelling of the land was confirmed by satellite surveying. The surveying equipment embedded in the mountain was pushed upward and outward each year. Hot spring temperatures rose from 69 to 83 degrees Celsius. That's the temperature at which eggs cook. For three years, something was clearly moving beneath Mount Paektu. We still don’t know exactly why the swelling stopped or how much molten magma remains. Because the mountain straddles the border, it’s difficult to conduct thorough research. So, will Mount Paektu erupt again? There have been over 30 recorded minor eruptions since 946. Records from 1702 show that 3cm of ash accumulated in Buryeong and Gyeongseong, Hamgyong-do. That is ash that fell directly onto our land. There hasn't been an explosion of that scale for over 1,000 years since 946. That is why scientists never take their eyes off it, checking if energy is building up again underground. Over 30 times in a thousand years averages out to about once every 30 years. Though most were very small eruptions. The Korea Meteorological Administration considers a large-scale eruption in the near future to be unlikely. However, it is clear that Mount Paektu is not a dead volcano. And if it does erupt, what’s scarier than fire is water. Two billion tons of water from Heaven Lake could mix with volcanic ash and pour into the valleys. Just like that day over a thousand years ago when the crater wall burst. Mud mixed with volcanic ash and water is called a lahar. Heavy and fast like concrete, it sweeps away houses and bridges along the valley.
