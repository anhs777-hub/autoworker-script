The experience of seeing scary animals like lions, tigers, and wolves right in front of your eyes. You've all probably experienced that at least once, right? But while watching the animals at the zoo, you sometimes wonder, "What would happen if those animals escaped?" It sounds like something that only happens abroad, but actually, there have been animals in Korea that escaped from the zoo. Let’s look at the top four saddest, yet terrifying cases of animals that managed to see the outside world after being confined for so long. 4th Place: The Elephant Escape at Seoul Children's Grand Park. On April 20, 2005, South Korea was turned upside down when six elephants escaped simultaneously from Seoul Children's Grand Park in Gwangjin-gu and roamed the city. At 3 PM, the elephants were practicing for a show when they were startled by a flock of pigeons flying up right in front of them and bolted out. The elephants reportedly wandered the streets for quite some time while still wearing their colorful performance outfits. Police, responding to the emergency, arrived with sirens blaring, but the already terrified elephants were further startled by the noise, and as they looked for an escape route, they began charging into houses and restaurants. It was a case where the wrong response made the situation worse. After turning off the sirens and letting the noise die down, the elephants calmed down as they accepted carrots from the trainers and were eventually able to return to the zoo. Fortunately, there were no major casualties, except for a middle-aged woman who suffered a head injury while trying to avoid the elephants. However, after the incident was reported, crowds of curious people flocked to the zoo. Under the title of a so-called " Atonement Performance," the park opened for free for four days, where the elephants had to "seek forgiveness" as if they were sinners. 3rd Place: The Escape of the Wolf, Ari. In August 2009 , a female wolf named Ari escaped from a wildlife zoo in the Forest Resources Research Institute near Gwangneung Forest in Pocheon, Gyeonggi-do. Ari escaped on August 25th at 10:40 AM. It is said that she slipped through an unlocked door while the zookeeper was feeding the animals and cleaning the enclosure. Eighty zoo employees, along with search police, conducted a massive two-day search, but finding a wolf in the Gwangneung Forest—an area four times the size of Yeouido—was no easy task. They tried various methods, such as placing her mate, Arang, in a mobile cage near where she was spotted to lure her out, but each attempt ended in failure. Due to the risk that the hungry wolf might descend into residential areas after dark and cause safety incidents, Ari was eventually shot dead near the summit of a mountain behind the zoo 28 hours after her escape. They had prepared anesthesia for a live capture, but because she did not enter the effective range, they had no choice but to use firearms. The zoo decided to display Ari as a taxidermy specimen, considering she was a native wolf of high conservation value. Number 2. Escape of Kkoma the Sun Bear. On December 6, 2010, a bear escaped from Seoul Grand Park in Gwacheon. The Sun Bear named Kkoma slipped out through a gate while the zookeeper was feeding it and fled 6km away to Mt. Cheonggye. To find Kkoma, which fled quickly by taking advantage of being the smallest and most agile bear species, 120 staff members, 200 police officers, and even a helicopter were mobilized, but its whereabouts remained unknown despite three days of intensive searching. In the end, a mountain climbing ban was even issued for Mt. Cheonggye. Kkoma, who had been roaming the entire mountain for about a week, must have been hungry, as it stole and ate ramen and snacks at a store near the peak of Isubong, and based on these traces, capture traps were intensively set up around the store. And nine days after its escape, Kkoma was caught in one of the traps. During this movie-like escape, Kkoma gained countless fans, and it is said that the phrase "Don't run away, Malayan bear," used by the MBC News Desk anchor at the time, even became a famous quote. The number of visitors to Seoul Grand Park tripled on the first weekend after Kkoma returned to the zoo. Number 1. Escape of Horongi the Puma. On September 18, 2018, a female puma named Horongi escaped from Daejeon O-World, a municipal zoo in Sajeong-dong, Jung-gu, Daejeon. Emergency disaster alerts were sent to nearby residents, and the evening commute for Daejeon citizens became a scene of terror. Around 9 AM, a zookeeper did not lock the cage after cleaning, and Horongi, who escaped through the open door, was found near a wooded hill in the zoo an hour later. A tranquilizer gun was fired for a live capture, but the attempt failed as the puma ran away while in a sedated state. Consequently, fire and police authorities shifted their focus to killing the puma, following the Daejeon Urban Corporation's safety management manual which states that predators can be shot dead depending on the situation ; they pursued the animal and found Horongi near the animal hospital inside the zoo at 9:44 PM, 4 hours and 30 minutes after its escape. And then, Horongi was shot dead on the spot. Although it was said to be an inevitable choice, controversy arose regarding the appropriateness of the decision, given that the puma was killed despite not having left the zoo's facilities. It was later revealed that the zoo's safety management plan was poorly followed, from staff scheduling to broken security cameras, leading to severe disciplinary action against the zoo director and team leaders. Most escaped animals are shot and killed because they are considered a danger to humans. It essentially means that innocent animals are the ones paying the price for human negligence and flawed surveillance systems. Humans are the ones who brought these animals to the zoo, caused them to escape, and ultimately killed them. It raises fundamental questions about whether zoos that exhibit wild animals should even exist. Hoping that there will be no more such tragic deaths, this has been Ranking School. Thank you.
